## 1. Arm B: find and silence a planted leak

A head `51 → 32 → 32 → 1` was trained on the 50 embedding components plus one planted input that, in training, was the standardised target plus noise (noise sd 0.3) and, at test time, is pure noise. All inputs are standardised and unnamed and the leak sits at a random position, so it can be found only from the weights. Chance of picking it is 1 in 51. The cheap reference is the input whose first-layer weight column has the largest norm.

| Run | Leak at | Model silenced | Found | Largest column norm | Found | Test R²: unrepaired | after model's edit | after column-norm edit | leak column zeroed (ideal) | trained without leak |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `loop_heat_capacity`, seed 0 | 10 | 10 | yes | 10 | yes | 0.23 | 0.36 | 0.36 | 0.36 | 0.63 |
| `loop_heat_capacity`, seed 1 | 46 | 46 | yes | 46 | yes | 0.33 | 0.62 | 0.62 | 0.62 | 0.72 |
| `loop_bulk_modulus`, seed 0 | 10 | 0 | no | 9 | no | 0.28 | −0.24 | 0.30 | 0.59 | 0.78 |
| `loop_bulk_modulus`, seed 1 | 46 | 50 | no | 20 | no | 0.57 | 0.56 | 0.57 | 0.77 | 0.82 |

Control, `loop_bulk_modulus` seed 0 with every tensor's entries shuffled (so no input column means anything): the model silenced input 48, used the same back-propagation-by-hand method, and stated 25 to 30% confidence, noting that "the margin is within what chance alone would produce across 51 columns". On the four real networks it stated 65%, 80 to 85%, 85% and 60 to 65%.

Reading:

- The model found the leak in two of four runs. Those are exactly the two runs in which the one-line column-norm statistic also finds it. Where the statistic fails, the model failed too.
- In the two failures the model did real arithmetic on the weights: it traced first-layer units through the next two layers and picked the input with the most coherent effect on the output. On `loop_bulk_modulus` seed 0 that input was a legitimate, strongly predictive embedding component, not the leak. The model stated 85% confidence and its edit took test R² from 0.28 to −0.24.
- The control behaved as it should: on shuffled weights the model's confidence dropped to 25 to 30% and it said the evidence looked like chance. Its confidence does track whether there is structure in the weights. It does not track whether its answer is right: the two wrong answers came with 85% and 60 to 65%, the two right ones with 80 to 85% and 65%.
- So the model does read weights here, unlike in arm C. What it cannot do from weights alone is tell a leak from a good feature: both show up as an input the network leans on. That needs data, or names.
- A wrong repair made with high stated confidence is the failure to design against. Any use of this in a loop needs a held-out check before an edit is accepted.

Reading:

- The three replies agree with each other. All three titanium networks are a beta-stabiliser score through a V-shaped curve; all three alloy-strength networks are a rule of mixtures plus an aluminium-driven hardening term. The model is writing down textbook metallurgy as weights, and does so repeatably.
- With the prior, ranking at 10 labels is higher on both tasks for all three replies, and the spread across seeds is two to six times smaller. At 20 labels the gain holds on alloy strength (0.42 → 0.58 to 0.59) and is small on titanium. From 50 labels the prior makes no difference or costs 0.01 to 0.04.
- R² with the fitted slope is unstable at 10 labels on titanium (−1.43, −0.56, −0.05 for the three replies against 0.06 without the prior). Ranking is what the prior improves; the scale is not.
- Still missing: the same test with a rule of mixtures written by a person, which is what would show whether the LLM adds anything beyond being a quick way to type in a handbook.

## 3. BO with the written prior

Pool-based BO on the two composition tasks (`experiments/e1_surrogate_head/run_bo.py`). Each run starts from 5 random labelled alloys and labels 25 more, one at a time, by expected improvement. Goals: maximise yield strength (pool of 722, best 3,416 MPa); minimise Young's modulus (pool of 237, best 45 GPa). Eight seeds; regret is the gap to the best alloy in the pool, mean over seeds.

The fixed-slope rule (`prior_one`: mean = m(x) + b) was added after the pilot showed the fitted slope is unstable on few labels; both are reported.

| Task | Arm | Regret after 10 | Regret after 25 | Seeds reaching the top 5% | Mean steps to reach it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mpea_yield_strength` (MPa) | random | 960 | 740 | 7 of 8 | 9.6 |
| | GP | **393** | 351 | 8 of 8 | 2.6 |
| | GP + prior, fitted slope: replies 1 / 2 / 3 | 486 / 712 / 770 | 327 / 489 / 502 | 8 / 8 / 8 | 3.0 / 4.4 / 4.0 |
| | GP + prior, slope one: replies 1 / 2 / 3 | 716 / 756 / 644 | 325 / 233 / 444 | 8 / 8 / 8 | 5.0 / 8.4 / 5.0 |
| | written network alone: replies 1 / 2 / 3 | 1,070 / 1,083 / 1,086 | 584 / 753 / 773 | 8 / 8 / 8 | 11.2 / 10.6 / 10.0 |
| `ti_alloy_youngs_modulus` (GPa) | random | 10.4 | 7.5 | 4 of 8 | 18.0 |
| | GP | 8.4 | 6.0 | 7 of 8 | 11.5 |
| | GP + prior, fitted slope: replies 1 / 2 / 3 | 9.5 / **5.0** / 8.5 | 5.5 / **0.9** / 5.0 | 6 / 8 / 6 | 13.9 / 8.4 / 14.5 |
| | GP + prior, slope one: replies 1 / 2 / 3 | 7.1 / 6.8 / 9.0 | 3.9 / 2.9 / 5.6 | 7 / 7 / 7 | 8.5 / 11.4 / 11.9 |
| | written network alone: replies 1 / 2 / 3 | 13.4 / **0.0** / 13.0 | 7.0 / **0.0** / 10.0 | 8 / 8 / 0 | 21.4 / 5.9 / 26.0 |

Final regret against the plain GP, seed by seed (better / equal / worse): alloy strength, fitted slope 2/6/0, 0/6/2, 1/6/1 and slope one 2/5/1, 3/4/1, 3/3/2; titanium, fitted slope 3/2/3, 8/0/0, 3/3/2 and slope one 5/1/2, 6/0/2, 2/3/3.

Reading:

- **The ranking gain of section 2 did not become a BO gain.** On alloy strength the prior made the first ten steps worse for every reply and both slope rules (regret 486 to 770 against 393), and after 25 steps the outcome is mixed. The plain GP already reaches the top 5% in under three steps there, so there was little room.
- **On titanium the outcome depends on which reply was used.** Reply 2 is better than the GP in all eight seeds with the fitted slope, and used alone it puts a best-in-pool alloy among its first picks (regret 0 in every seed within ten steps). Replies 1 and 3 are indistinguishable from the GP, and reply 3 used alone never reaches the top 5%. The three replies looked the same in section 2 (ρ 0.50 to 0.55); what separates them is how they order the few lowest-modulus alloys, which an average rank correlation does not see.
- So one reply in three produced a clear benefit on one task of two. With this spread across replies, a claim needs many replies per task and a way to choose among them without labels. An obvious candidate, not tested: write several, and let the first few labels pick.
- Fixing the slope at one is not consistently better than fitting it.

## 4. E4: do fitted surrogate parameters predict ranking quality?

27,583 fitted GP-family surrogates (gp, mtgp, dgp, mtdgp, decoupled_dgp) from `autoresearch-bo`, 22 benchmarks. Target: the surrogate's Spearman correlation at that iteration. A gradient-boosted model predicts it for a benchmark it never saw. `experiments/e4_surrogate_zoo/run_zoo.py`.

| Inputs | R² | MAE | ρ of prediction with truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| training-set mean | −0.14 | 0.387 | |
| context: iteration, training size, input dimension, surrogate family | 0.15 | 0.303 | 0.517 |
| fitted parameters: lengthscale summary, output scale, noise, mean constant, task correlation | 0.23 | 0.289 | 0.508 |
| both | 0.23 | 0.287 | 0.517 |

Means over the 22 held-out benchmarks. Adding parameters to context lowers MAE on 17 of 22 benchmarks and raises ρ on 13 of 22.

Reading:

- The fitted parameters carry some information about how well a surrogate ranks on an unseen problem: R² rises from 0.15 to 0.23 and the error falls by 5%. They do not improve the ordering of surrogates (ρ unchanged).
- The effect is concentrated: `car_side_impact` (MAE 0.290 → 0.186) and `vehicle_safety` (0.266 → 0.174) account for much of it; on `penicillin`, `mpea_strength` and `noisy_dtlz2` there is none.
- This is a weak signal, enough to justify step 3 (warm-starting refits) only as a cheap try, not as a main line. The parameter table has no rows for the tree and neural surrogates, so the analysis says nothing about them.
