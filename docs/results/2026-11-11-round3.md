## 2. BO surrogate: BO with a prior chosen by the first labels

Rule fixed and committed before the run (`run_bo_select.py`, commit 4de5c4a): at each step take, among an arm's candidate priors, the one most correlated (Pearson) with the labels so far; below 0.3 use no prior. Pool-based BO, 5 initial labels, 25 steps, 20 seeds. Area under the regret curve; paired Wilcoxon against the plain GP over seeds, Holm-corrected within task. Full table with regret at 5, 10, 25: `results/bo_select/summary_init5_steps25_seeds20.md`.

| Task (unit) | GP | Handbook | Opus 5.5 | Fable 5.2 | Sonnet 5.5 | Haiku 5.5 | All replies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ti_alloy_youngs_modulus` (GPa) | 7.68 | 7.02 | **5.55** (p 0.004) | 6.92 | 7.34 | 7.61 | **5.41** (0.004) |
| `ti_alloy_yield_strength` (MPa) | 314 | 327 | **153** (0.047) | **148** (0.026) | **216** (0.043) | 255 | **139** (0.026) |
| `ti_alloy_hardness` (HV) | 40.1 | 40.1 | **5.95** (0.003) | **10.0** (0.003) | **11.3** (0.003) | 27.2 | **6.13** (0.003) |

Bold: lower than the GP with Holm-corrected p < 0.05. Random search is worse than the GP on every task (area 8.37, 392, 66.9, 338, 1,010).

Reading:

- Opus 5.5 priors make BO significantly better on four of five tasks, Fable 5.2 on three, Sonnet 5.5 on two, Haiku 5.5 on none. Final regret with the Opus prior: titanium modulus 1.6 GPa against 5.6; titanium yield strength 17 MPa against 114; alloy hardness 94 HV against 112.
- **One task goes the other way.** On `mpea_yield_strength` the Opus and Fable arms are worse than the GP (4 seeds better, 14 worse; uncorrected p 0.03 and 0.02, not significant after correction), although the same networks improved static ranking on that task in section 1. Round 2 saw the same thing with different seeds. Static ranking gain did not predict BO gain here; the cause is not diagnosed. A candidate: the best alloys in that pool are outliers that the smooth written trend ranks low, so the prior steers expected improvement away from them.
- Pooling every model's replies and letting the labels choose is as good as the best single model on four tasks, so the selection rule does protect against the weak models' replies.
- The round-2 worry (one reply in three helps) is resolved for the stronger models: with selection, the benefit is consistent across 20 seeds.

## 3. MLIP: editing MACE's readout

Setup. `MACE-MPA-0 medium`; reference bulk moduli K_VRH for 400 Materials Project structures (matminer `elastic_tensor_2015`, at most 12 atoms). MACE relaxes each structure, seven volumes at ±3%, cubic fit. The 128 scalar features entering each readout are cached per structure and strain, so the energy of any edited readout is exact (unedited weights reproduce MACE to 1e-14 eV; `src/wsp/mace_readout.py`). Split in half; five seeds for baselines, seed 0 for the LLM arm.

On the 15 structures with our own VASP bulk moduli MACE is +26.4 GPa (8%) too stiff, MAE 35.0 GPa. On the 400 it is +6.4 GPa too stiff, MAE 16.5 ± 0.6 GPa, ρ 0.96. Neither set shows the softening reported for universal potentials.

Baselines on the test half (five seeds):

| Method | MAE, GPa | Bias, GPa | Energy change, meV/atom |
|---|---|---|---|
| unedited | 16.45 ± 0.62 | +6.36 | 0 |
| one global scale | 15.61 ± 0.71 | −1.81 | 263 |
| gradient fit of 2,193 readout weights | 12.19 ± 0.62 | +2.13 | 125 |
| the same with an energy penalty of 1 | 16.15 ± 0.68 | +5.67 | 0.34 |

Trade-off of the gradient fit against the energy penalty (seed 0): MAE 12.4 at 25 meV/atom; 12.7 at 13.4; 13.9 at 5.2; 14.4 at 2.9; 15.1 at 1.4; 15.7 at 0.6. The bulk-modulus error can be bought down only by moving energies.

LLM arm, no forward passes. The prompt gives the formulas, all 2,283 numbers, the error by range of K, a limit of 5 meV/atom on the mean energy change, and a table of each `w0` weight's effect on mean K and mean energy.

Reading:

- **Prompt v1 was my error.** Its bulk-modulus column was computed at fixed volume. Checked afterwards against finite differences, it has the right sign for only 9 of 16 weights (correlation 0.26), because an edit shifts the equilibrium volume and K depends strongly on volume. Every v1 reply, and the closed-form solve from that table, made the bias worse. The prompt disclosed that the column was first-order; no model could correct for it without running the network.
- **With the correct table (v2)** Opus 5.5 produced three edits that stay inside the energy limit and reduce the bias by a quarter. Fable 5.2 did slightly more and landed at or just over the limit. Sonnet 4.6 and Haiku 5.5 removed more bias by ignoring the energy limit.
- What the good replies did is a constrained linear solve by hand over a table of 128 sensitivities: choose weights whose K effects add and whose energy effects cancel. That is arithmetic on a table the harness computed with 128 refits, not reading the weights.
- The gradient fit is better at the same energy cost (MAE 14.4 against 15.8). The LLM edit is valid and cheap, and not competitive.
- The physics result stands apart from the LLM: MACE's bulk-modulus error cannot be fixed in the readout without changing energies, so it lives mostly in the message-passing layers.
- Not run: edits with a forward-pass budget, which is the setting where the original work reported its large-model results.

## 4. BO surrogate: leak repair across models

Eleven networks (six bulk-modulus runs, five heat-capacity runs), each with one planted leak among 51 unnamed inputs. Two statistics that use only the weights set the bar: the largest first-layer column norm finds the leak in 4 of 11, and the largest linear path gain (`|w3·W2·W1|`, every ReLU open) in 6 of 11. In five of the six bulk-modulus networks the leak ranks second by gain, behind a legitimate dominant embedding component.

Model runs: 4 from round 2 (Fable 5.2: 2 of 4 found); 18 Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 runs launched and not yet scored; Sonnet and Haiku prompts generated and not launched. `python scripts/pending_runs.py` lists what is outstanding; this section is to be completed from `llm_arm.py score` on each run.

## 5. Generative model: arithmetic on RL-fine-tuned MatterGen weights

Task vector τ = fine-tuned − base, from the published loop's ungated MatterGen runs (five seeds each for bulk modulus and heat capacity). Analysis of all 20 MatterGen checkpoints (`task_vectors.py`): ‖τ‖ is 0.4 to 0.6% of the base norm; holding 90% of a weight matrix's change takes 32% of full rank at the median (48% for the base weights); cosine between seeds of the same target and policy averages 0.21, between targets 0.11 to 0.17.

Eleven variants were sampled on FASTER (128 crystals each, the same noise seed for all) and every crystal scored with the loop's own oracles, eSEN-30M-OAM bulk modulus and phonon heat capacity (`hprc/`, job 3257271, 2,975 SU). Bulk modulus was returned for 99 to 100% of crystals; heat capacity for 47 to 64% (the oracle rejects structures with imaginary modes).

Heat-capacity differences from base: mean of five seeds +0.051 [−0.008, +0.115], uncorrected p 0.012, Holm 0.11; no heat-capacity difference survives correction except the drop for the 3× bulk-modulus variant (−0.063, Holm p 0.0007). Full tables: `results/variants_summary.md`.

Reading:

- **The weight changes carry a direction that survives averaging.** The mean of five seeds' changes raises bulk modulus more than a single run's (+36 against +23 GPa) with a smaller change in the weights. The low cosine between seeds (0.21) had suggested the opposite; most of each run's change is noise that averaging removes.
- **Scaling works.** Three times the mean change gives mean K of 172 GPa and 35% of crystals above 200 GPa, against 2% for the base, with 127 distinct formulas in 128 samples. No further RL and no oracle calls were needed to get from the fine-tuned runs to this generator. Heat capacity falls and mean atomic mass rises with it, as expected for stiff heavy compounds.
- **Low rank is enough.** Rank 8 per weight matrix keeps +20 of the +23 GPa, so a LoRA-sized adapter could carry what the full-parameter RL run learned.
- **Negation** moves K down by 10 GPa, not significant at this sample size.
- **Addition does not interfere.** Adding the heat-capacity change leaves the bulk-modulus gain intact (+30 to +33 GPa). The heat-capacity effect itself is too weak here to say the sum achieves both: the heat-capacity runs shift chemistry toward lighter elements (mean atomic mass 103 → 88 to 91) and raise mean C_p by 0.02 to 0.05 J/g/K, short of significance with about 70 valid crystals per variant.
- Limits: one backbone (MatterGen), one sampling seed, 128 crystals per variant, no stability filter (energy above hull was not computed), and the loop's oracle rather than DFT.

## 6. Where the campaign stands

| Idea | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| LLM-written prior mean for the BO surrogate | 60 replies, 4 models, 5 tasks; BO with 20 seeds and corrected tests | Works with the strongest models on 4 of 5 tasks; hurts on one. The main positive result of the narrow sense. |
| LLM edit of a trained surrogate head | rounds 1 and 2 | Recalibration recipe, not weight reading. |
| LLM leak repair from weights | round 2 (2 of 4); 11-network benchmark in progress | Open. The bar is 6 of 11 from a one-line statistic. |
| LLM edit of an MLIP readout | 24 replies, 5 models, exact scoring, gradient trade-off curve | Feasible only with a harness-computed sensitivity table; below a gradient fit. |
| Task arithmetic on generator weights (broad sense) | 11 variants × 128 crystals with the loop's oracles | Works for bulk modulus: averaging, scaling and rank truncation all behave. The strongest result of the campaign. |
| Learning from the surrogate zoo | round 2 | Weak signal. |

What would make each positive result publishable:

1. Written priors: a second pool per task or a real closed loop; diagnose the `mpea_yield_strength` failure; non-Anthropic models; priors on structure-based surrogates, where inputs have no names.
2. Generator arithmetic: stability and novelty filters, more seeds of sampling, CrystalFlow and ADiT, the scaling curve (1×, 2×, 3×, 5×), and DFT on the top crystals of the 3× variant.
