# Round 6: joint prior mean in BoTorch, and a weight edit of an open table reader

Date: 2026-11-11. Raw runs: `experiments/e1_surrogate_head/results/joint_mean/`, `experiments/e6_extraction_reader/`. Commands in `REPRODUCE.md` section 4f. Still running when this page was written: Thermo-Calc tasks (E7), CrystalFlow and ADiT task vectors (E3), the open reader on real papers (E6); see `PROGRESS.md`.

## 1. The written network in the GP mean, fitted jointly

Until now the mean a·m(x) + b was fitted in two stages: a and b by least squares on the labels, then a constant-mean GP on the residuals. `src/wsp/joint_mean.py` adds two joint fits with BoTorch's `SingleTaskGP` and the same input kernel:

- **learned:** GPyTorch `LinearMean` on the network's output; a and b are hyperparameters fitted with the kernel by marginal likelihood (start a = 1, b = 0; weak Normal priors).
- **integrated:** the mean is the network's output and the kernel has an extra linear term on it plus a constant, which is the exact marginal over Gaussian a and b. Their uncertainty reaches the error bars.

**Static test.** Five alloy tasks, every valid Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 reply, five seeds (120 cells per label count). No fit failed.

**In the BO loop.** Same seeds, candidates and first-label selection rule as round 3; 20 seeds, 25 steps; area under the regret curve relative to the plain GP, mean over the ten task × model cells: two-stage 0.69, learned 0.70, integrated 0.68. Per task and model (`results/joint_mean/bo_summary_seeds20.md`) no joint arm differs from the two-stage fit at p < 0.05 (paired Wilcoxon, uncorrected); the closest are Ti modulus with the Opus prior, where both joint arms are worse (5 better / 14 worse, p = 0.06), and Ti yield strength with the Opus prior, where the integrated arm is better (11 / 8, p = 0.06). The task that fails with a written prior, MPEA yield strength, fails with all three.

- **Integrating a and b out repairs the calibration fault reported in round 4** at the label counts where it was worst: coverage at 5 labels 0.63 → 0.79 and the likelihood is the best of the four arms, with slightly better ranking.
- **It does not change the optimisation result.** Regret is the same within noise. The gain of the written prior comes from its ranking; the error bars matter less for expected improvement on these pools.
- The learned-slope version is no better than two-stage from 10 labels on: a and b are still point estimates.
- Recommendation for the method as described in the paper: the integrated form. It is one model, fitted once, and its intervals are honest at 5 labels.
- The absolute regret numbers of this run differ by a few percent from round 3's table because this script averages the regret curve including step 0; the comparisons here are all computed the same way.

## 2. A weight edit of an open-weights table reader (E6)

**Question.** ORCHID extracts thermodynamic tables from CALPHAD papers with Claude Opus 5.5 among its readers. Can a small open model be edited, in its weights, toward that level?

**Test set.** ORCHID-Bench (github.com/orchid-calphad/orchid-bench, commit 584d34a; code MIT, documents CC BY 4.0): 20 synthetic papers with exact truth keys, 81 table crops, as degraded 150 dpi scans. Crops use the truth key's region, as the benchmark's own OCR baseline does. Scoring is the benchmark's `score_tables` (cell text must match exactly).

**Readers.** Open model: GLM-OCR (zai-org/GLM-OCR, 0.9 billion weights, MIT, pinned revision), prompt "Table Recognition:". Baseline: Opus 5.5 as a blind reader of the same crop images (`opus_reader.py`, 20 sessions, tool use audited).

| Reader, all 20 papers (2,740 cells) | Cells exact | Ignoring spaces | Numeric cells: value exact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opus 5.5 | 97.4% | 97.4% | 100% |
| GLM-OCR as shipped | 72.2% | 81.3% | 100% |
| Tesseract (benchmark's `ocr_table_cells`, from its RESULTS.md) | 16.4% | | |

The open model reads every number. Its gap to Opus is typography: dropped spaces (`<27` for `< 27`, `T(K)` for `T (K)`), hyphens for en dashes, flattened superscripts in unit headers (`mol-1`), `v` for `ν`, LaTeX wrappers.

**The edit.** The smallest edit the output layer admits: a bias per vocabulary entry added to the logits (59,392 numbers, zero as shipped). The prompt (`edit_arm.py`) lists the wrong cells on the 10 training papers with both token sequences and how often each token occurs in correct cells. The model replies with bias entries. Scored on the 10 held-out papers (1,342 cells):

| Reader | Bias entries | Cells exact | Ignoring spaces | Cells matching the printed text among numeric cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GLM-OCR as shipped | 0 | 71.4% | 80.8% | 80.2% |
| + text clean-up rule fitted on training cells (no weight edit) | 0 | 71.6% | 81.1% | 80.2% |
| + bias from error counts (±2 on tokens over-represented on one side) | 69 | 74.1% | 80.5% | 80.2% |
| + bias written by Opus 5.5, reply 1 | 53 | 73.3% | 80.3% | 80.4% |
| + bias written by Opus 5.5, reply 2 | 45 | 74.7% | 80.2% | 80.2% |
| + bias written by Fable 5.2, reply 1 | 46 | 75.8% | 81.4% | 80.2% |
| + bias written by Fable 5.2, reply 2 | 58 | **76.9%** | 82.0% | 80.5% |
| Opus 5.5 reading the crops (all 20 papers) | | 97.4% | | 100% |

(The last column of this table is string equality of numeric cells, which also fails on a missing space or a different minus sign; the benchmark's value-exact metric is 100% for the unedited model.)

- **The written edits work and do no harm:** +2 to +5.5 points of exact cells, numbers untouched. Both models reasoned from the token table, pushed down merged no-space tokens and raised their spaced counterparts, and deliberately left digits, the hyphen and superscript bytes alone to protect the numbers.
- **They are level with the no-LLM bias from error counts** (74.1%); Fable's two replies are 1.7 to 2.8 points above it, Opus's are at or below it. Four replies do not establish a difference.
- **They close about a fifth of the gap to Opus at best.** A context-free bias cannot decide where a space belongs, and the remaining errors are structural (merged header cells) or need multi-token changes.
- **Not run:** a gradient-trained bias (the fair gradient baseline for this arm), LoRA fine-tuning on Opus's transcriptions (the route that should close the gap; it is weight-space learning, not programming), edits with feedback, and other open models.

**Real papers.** `build_real_set.py` samples 400 tables from the 2,068 tables of the public ORCHID release that have at least four accepted multi-reader values with Opus among the readers and a local PDF (at most three per paper, split by paper), and re-renders the crops with the pipeline's rule. On a six-table smoke test the unedited open model recovered 199 of 199 labelled values. The full run is in progress; crops and replies stay in `data/large/orchid_real/` and are not committed (publisher material).
