# Round 7: written networks on Thermo-Calc targets and on crystal structures

Date: 2026-11-12. Raw results: `experiments/e7_calphad_prior/results/` (overview in `overview.md`), `experiments/e1_surrogate_head/results/structure_prior/`. Commands in `REPRODUCE.md` sections 4f and 4g. Both sets of GP fits ran on FASTER (jobs 3260503 and 3260506). This page covers four Thermo-Calc systems; five more systems have complete pools and their written networks are being collected.

## 1. Thermo-Calc targets (E7)

**Setup.** Four alloy systems (Cantor + Al, Ni superalloy, refractory high-entropy, steel), 200 to 400 compositions each, computed with TC-Python 2025b. 16 targets: solidus, freezing range and phase fractions. Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 each wrote a small network per target with no data, once from knowledge alone and once with phase-diagram facts for the binary and ternary sub-systems in the prompt (2 or 3 replies each). The no-LLM comparison is an extrapolation from the same sub-system facts. Each prior enters the GP mean as a·m(x) + b.

**Ranking on the held-out half** (mean Spearman ρ over 16 targets, two-stage fit):

| Prior | Alone, no labels | 5 labels | 10 labels | 20 labels | 50 labels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| None (plain GP) | n/a | 0.37 | 0.56 | 0.69 | 0.81 |
| Sub-system extrapolation (no LLM) | 0.76 | 0.71 | 0.74 | 0.78 | 0.85 |
| Opus 5.5 | 0.73 | 0.67 | 0.72 | 0.77 | 0.84 |
| Opus 5.5 + facts | 0.79 | 0.72 | 0.75 | 0.78 | 0.84 |
| Fable 5.2 | 0.71 | 0.66 | 0.69 | 0.75 | 0.83 |
| Fable 5.2 + facts | 0.76 | 0.69 | 0.74 | 0.76 | 0.83 |

**BO** (expected improvement, 5 initial labels, 25 steps, 20 seeds; area under the regret curve relative to the plain GP; Holm-corrected Wilcoxon; 15 targets, one left out because the plain GP already has zero regret):

| Prior | Significantly better / worse / no difference | Median relative regret area |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-system extrapolation (no LLM) | 8 / 0 / 7 | 0.51 |
| Opus 5.5 | 7 / 0 / 8 | 0.44 |
| Opus 5.5 + facts | 7 / 0 / 8 | 0.49 |
| Fable 5.2 | 6 / 1 / 8 | 0.47 |
| Fable 5.2 + facts | 7 / 0 / 8 | 0.47 |

- Every prior about halves BO regret, and at 5 labels raises ρ from 0.37 to 0.66–0.72.
- A network written with no facts does about as well in BO as the extrapolation that uses computed sub-system data.
- Facts in the prompt improve the network alone (Opus 0.73 to 0.79) but change BO little.
- The significant wins are on solidus and freezing range. Most phase-fraction targets show no difference; on several the plain GP's regret is already near zero.
- One significant loss: Fable without facts on the Ni γ′ fraction (1.82 times the plain GP's regret area).
- The two-stage fit costs calibration at 5 labels (95% coverage about 0.75 against 0.88); the joint fits keep 0.89–0.90 with similar ranking.
- Rule of mixtures is constant for systems with a balance element, so it is reported only for the high-entropy systems.

## 2. Crystal structures: bulk modulus and heat capacity (E1)

**Setup.** The closed-loop surrogates see a crystal as 50 unnamed embedding coordinates, so a model cannot write a network on them. It can on 15 named descriptors of the same crystal. The 15 → 16 → 1 networks written earlier for the generator filter (no data, no new model runs) were evaluated on every loop structure and used as the prior mean of the embedding GP. Train rows come from one run's warm-start pool, test rows are the structures the generator proposed in that run. Rule with no LLM: 1 / volume per atom for bulk modulus, Dulong–Petit (3R / mean atomic mass) for heat capacity.

**Bulk modulus** (6 runs × 3 draws; ρ on generated structures, best-tenth recall in brackets):

| Arm | Alone | 5 labels | 10 | 20 | 50 | all 500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain GP on the embedding | n/a | 0.15 (0.19) | 0.57 (0.39) | 0.82 (0.55) | 0.85 (0.63) | 0.88 (0.69) |
| GP on embedding + descriptors (no LLM) | n/a | 0.30 | 0.37 | 0.52 | 0.49 | 0.66 |
| Rule 1/volume, two-stage | 0.88 | 0.77 (0.44) | 0.89 (0.51) | 0.93 (0.64) | 0.93 (0.70) | 0.92 (0.78) |
| Opus 5.5 network, learned a, b | 0.95 | 0.95 (0.65) | 0.94 (0.69) | 0.90 (0.66) | 0.90 (0.69) | 0.90 (0.71) |
| Fable 5.2 network, learned a, b | 0.93 | 0.93 (0.75) | 0.92 (0.75) | 0.90 (0.70) | 0.90 (0.67) | 0.92 (0.70) |
| Sonnet 5.5 network, learned a, b | 0.88 | 0.88 (0.66) | 0.88 (0.66) | 0.90 (0.67) | | |
| Haiku 5.5 network, learned a, b | 0.66 | 0.66 (0.56) | 0.70 (0.58) | 0.79 (0.59) | | |

- With 5 or 10 labels the Opus and Fable networks beat the plain GP in all 18 cells (p = 8e-6) and beat the 1/volume rule. They rank better alone (0.95, 0.93) than the plain GP does with all 500 labels (0.88).
- From 20 labels on the rule's two-stage fit is as good or better, and with the full pool no prior differs from the plain GP.
- The learned and integrated fits keep 95% coverage at 0.97–0.99 with 5 labels; the two-stage fit drops to 0.69–0.73.
- Adding the raw descriptors to the GP inputs hurts at every size above 5 labels. The gain comes from the network's knowledge, not from the extra columns.
- Haiku's networks are no better than the plain GP from 10 labels on.

**Heat capacity.** Dulong–Petit alone ranks the generated structures at ρ = 1.00, and so do the Opus, Fable and Sonnet networks (Haiku 0.98). Every prior lifts the GP from 0.15 to 1.00 at 5 labels. The task is solved by a one-line rule, so the written network adds nothing over it; this belongs with the cases where a rule already works.

**The network's hidden layer as GP inputs** (`run_structure_features.py`; bulk modulus, ρ with best-tenth recall in brackets):

| Labels | Model | Hidden units only | Hidden + prior mean | Embedding + hidden | Embedding + hidden + prior mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Opus | 0.43 (0.34) | 0.93 (0.63) | 0.39 (0.25) | 0.95 (0.65) |
| 20 | Opus | 0.76 (0.55) | 0.93 (0.57) | 0.80 (0.62) | 0.92 (0.66) |
| 50 | Opus | 0.86 (0.62) | 0.94 (0.62) | 0.87 (0.68) | 0.92 (0.67) |
| 500 | Opus | 0.94 (0.65) | 0.95 (0.66) | 0.87 (0.77) | 0.89 (0.77) |
| 500 | Fable | 0.94 (0.74) | 0.93 (0.69) | 0.89 (0.73) | 0.91 (0.77) |

- As inputs alone the 16 hidden units need labels like any other features (0.43 at 5 labels). The gain with few labels comes from the output used as the mean.
- With the full pool a GP on the 16 written hidden units ranks better than the GP on the 50 embedding coordinates (0.94 against 0.88) and better than the plain GP on embedding plus raw descriptors (0.66). The written hidden layer is a useful learned-free feature map.
- Hidden units as inputs with the output as the mean gives 0.93–0.95 at every label count, the most stable arm.

## 3. What this changes

- The written network is a usable property predictor on three kinds of input: experimental alloy compositions (rounds 1–6), computed Thermo-Calc targets, and crystal-structure descriptors.
- Its clearest use is the few-label regime (5 to 20 labels). With hundreds of labels the plain GP catches up.
- Where a simple rule already ranks the target (heat capacity, solidus by sub-system extrapolation) the network matches the rule and does not beat it.
