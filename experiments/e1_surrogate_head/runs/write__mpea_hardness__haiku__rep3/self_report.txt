I reasoned from general knowledge of multi-principal-element alloys without computation. Refractory elements (Hf, Mo, Nb, Re, Ta, V, W, Zr) and Ti/Cr get positive weight in a hidden unit that drives hardness up, interstitial and strong-solid-solution elements (B, C, Al, Si) get a second positive unit, and soft FCC stabilisers (Co, Cu, Fe, Ni, Pd) feed a third unit through a negative weight and a bias of 1, so it turns on for soft-element-poor alloys. The output adds these with weights 300, 150 and 50 and a bias of 150, giving a rough scale of several hundred HV. Confidence that the trends are right: about 45%, and the absolute scale is less certain.
