# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I built the network by hand as a rule-of-mixtures model: hidden unit 0 is a linear sum whose per-element coefficients are my recalled solid-solution hardening strengths in HV per unit atomic fraction (pure Ti about 210 HV; Si, Fe, Mn, Cr, Mo strongest; Nb, Ta, Hf, Zr weak), since the fractions sum to 1 and all coefficients are positive so the ReLU passes it through. Units 1-3 form a tent-shaped bump of up to about 40 HV on a Mo-equivalent-like beta-stabiliser sum, standing for omega/alpha-double-prime hardening in lean metastable beta alloys, and units 4-10 are hinges that flatten the slope for Al, Fe, Si, Cr, Mn, Cu and Mo above a threshold so that concentrated alloys do not extrapolate too high. I checked a few alloys by mental arithmetic (Ti-15Mo about 310 HV, Ti-35Nb about 265 HV, Ti-6Al-4V about 320 HV, TiZrNbTa about 430 HV); nothing was fitted to data, and I expect the scale to be roughly right (maybe 60 percent confident the mean error is under about 60 HV) with trends more reliable than absolute values.
