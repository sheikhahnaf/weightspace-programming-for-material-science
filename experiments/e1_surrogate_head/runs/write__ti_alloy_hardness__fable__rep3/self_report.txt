I built the network by hand as a solid-solution-hardening model: hidden unit 0 is an always-positive linear mix giving 200 HV for pure Ti plus a per-element hardening coefficient (strong for Fe, Si, Mn, Cr, Cu and interstitial/other elements; moderate for Mo, Al, V, Sn; weak for Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf), scaled by an output weight of 100. Units 1 to 8 are single-element hinge units with negative output weights that reduce the slope above a threshold fraction, so the strong hardeners saturate instead of extrapolating linearly; the remaining units are left at zero. Coefficients were set from remembered anchor alloys (CP Ti about 200 HV, Ti-6Al-4V about 320-340 HV, Ti-15Mo about 300 HV, Ti-35Nb about 260 HV, Ti-5Fe about 340 HV), with no computation beyond mental arithmetic. Nothing had to be found, so no confidence percentage applies; I expect the scale and sign of trends to be right but not effects of phase constitution such as omega-phase hardening.
