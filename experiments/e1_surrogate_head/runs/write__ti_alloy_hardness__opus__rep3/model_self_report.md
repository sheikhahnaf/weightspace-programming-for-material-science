# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> Hidden unit 0 is a rule of mixtures that is always positive. Each element gets an effective hardness, about 190 HV for Ti, with large values for the strong strengtheners Fe, Si, Cr, Mn, Mo, Cu and Al, and small values for Nb, Ta, Zr, Hf and V. I set them so that CP-Ti, Ti-6Al-4V, Ti-6Al-7Nb, Ti-13Nb-13Zr, TMZF, Ti-15Mo and TNZT fall at their typical 190-340 HV. Units 1-4 compute a Mo-equivalent beta-stability index in atomic fractions, with Al counted as negative. From it they build a triangular bump of about +100 HV that peaks near 5 at% Mo-eq, for athermal omega near the beta-retention limit, and a softening slope above about 7.5 at% for stable beta. Units 5-7 add saturations: Al above 13 at%, so TiAl/Ti3Al stay near 330-360 HV; Nb+Ta above 10 at%, so beta Ti-Nb alloys fall back to about 200 HV; and a small hardening term for alloys with less than 65 at% Ti.
