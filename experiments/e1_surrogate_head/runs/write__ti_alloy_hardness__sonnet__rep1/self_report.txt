I used one hidden unit that acts as a linear model, y = sum of v_i * x_i. Because the fractions sum to 1, each v_i is the hardness of a pure-element end member. Titanium is set to about 220 HV. Si, Cr, Fe, Al, Cu and Mn are set high (600 to 900 HV) for solid-solution and intermetallic hardening. Mo, Sn, Nb, Ta, V, Hf and Zr are set lower (250 to 450 HV). All values come from general materials knowledge, with no calculation. I have no fitted data, so I would put confidence in the overall scale at about 50% and in the trends at about 60%.
