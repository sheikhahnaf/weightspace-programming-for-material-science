Hidden unit 0 is a rule of mixtures that is always positive. Each element gets an effective modulus contribution: Ti 108, Al 170, Mo 150, Fe 150, Nb 90, Ta 120, Zr 65, Sn 40, Si 200 GPa, and so on. Units 1 and 2 share one beta-stabilizer index, a molybdenum equivalent in atomic percent (Mo 1, Fe 1.7, Mn 0.9, Cr 0.8, Ta 0.4, V 0.36, Nb 0.27, Sn 0.5, Cu 0.2, other 0.3, Al -0.15). Together they make a V-shaped dip in modulus. E falls by about 7 GPa per at% Mo-eq until the beta-retention threshold near 6 at%, where E is about 60-65 GPa. Above that it rises again by about 2.5 GPa per at% as the beta phase becomes more stable. Before finalizing, I checked the result by hand against known alloys: CP-Ti about 108, Ti-6Al-4V about 114, Ti-13Nb-13Zr about 88, Ti-15Mo about 73 and TNZT/TNTZ about 60-63 GPa.
