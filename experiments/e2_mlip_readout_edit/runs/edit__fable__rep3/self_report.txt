I ranked the w0 weights by |dK mean| divided by the standard deviation of dE across crystals, took the 14 best, and moved each by an amount proportional to dK/sd(dE)^2 in the direction that lowers the bulk modulus, which is the minimum-energy-scatter way to buy a given bulk modulus change if the per-weight energy scatters are independent. The summed first-order mean energy change of those edits (+10.8 meV per atom) is cancelled with scale_shift.shift = -0.0108 eV, since shift adds a constant per atom without touching the bulk modulus. By hand arithmetic the edit should lower the bulk modulus by about 1.6 GPa on average with a crystal-to-crystal energy scatter of about 4.6 meV per atom (mean absolute change about 3.7 meV); I deliberately did not try to remove the full +4.7 GPa, because under the same independence assumption that would cost roughly 13 meV of scatter and breach the 5 meV limit. I am about 65% confident the bulk modulus error falls on new crystals while the energy stays within 5 meV; the main risks are correlated energy scatter between the edited features and the neglected equilibrium-volume shift.
