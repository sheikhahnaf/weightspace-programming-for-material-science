The large w0 weights follow dK ~ -40 x dE_mean, which looks like a scale-like "binding" mode. Its per-crystal energy residuals probably add up coherently, so I left those weights alone (117 in particular). I worked out the excess stiffness dK + mu*dE_mean for each weight. Then I picked the 11 weights with the best excess-dK per unit of energy sd (75, 39, 12, 58, 54, 114, 62, 83, 3, 89, 19) and set the change in each in proportion to (excess dK)/sd^2, using a Lagrange multiplier (mu ~ 50) so the predicted mean energy changes cancel (~0.1 meV/atom, so no shift edit was needed). Assuming independent residuals and linear response, the edit is predicted to lower the bulk modulus by about 1.7 GPa on average (bias +4.7 -> ~+3.0 GPa), with an energy-per-atom residual sd of about 4.5 meV (mean |dE| ~3.6 meV). I am about 65% confident that it both lowers the bulk-modulus error on new crystals and stays under the 5 meV energy limit. The main risk is unknown correlations between the weights' per-crystal energy changes.
