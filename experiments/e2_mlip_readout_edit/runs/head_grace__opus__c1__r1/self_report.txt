A single weight cannot move the mean bulk modulus by more than about 0.1 to 0.2 GPa within the 5 meV/atom limit. So I combined 13 weights whose bulk-modulus effect is large compared with how much their energy effect varies from crystal to crystal (mostly the small-activation units 30, 37, 46, 7, 24, 6, 39, 38, 48, 52, 3, 17 and 50), each moved in the direction that lowers K. I sized each change in proportion to dK_mean / dE_sd^2 and lowered weight 31, a nearly uniform feature, by 0.026 so that the mean energy shift cancels. Assuming the residual energy changes are independent, the expected effect is about -0.4 GPa on mean K with a mean absolute energy change of about 3 to 4 meV/atom. This attempt is also a probe: because energy is linear in w, the measured energy change tells me how far later attempts can scale this direction. I am about 55% confident that this lowers the MAE at all, and if it does the gain will be small (hundredths of a GPa), because most of the 5.8 GPa error is crystal-specific rather than a bias.
