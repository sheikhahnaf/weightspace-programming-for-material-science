I started from the physical rule that bulk modulus scales as cohesive energy density, B ~ 1.2 * T_eff / V, where T_eff is the mean elemental melting point corrected for elements whose elemental melting point misrepresents their bonding (bonuses for C/N/O, S/Se/P, 4d/5d metals, p-block metals, metalloids and electronegativity; penalties for alkali and H), and I checked the constants by hand against remembered values for about 40 solids (W, Fe, Cu, Au, Pt, MgO, Al2O3, TiO2, NaCl, LiF, ZnS, TiN, and others). Since a one-hidden-layer ReLU net cannot multiply, I built T_eff * s(V) from six pairs of units, relu(T_eff + K(Vj - V)) - relu(K(Vj - V)), each of which switches on an extra T_eff slope below a volume threshold (23, 17, 13.5, 11, 9, 7 A3/atom), giving a piecewise-linear staircase approximation of 1.2/V, plus a base unit, a saturation unit for very high T_eff, and two penalty units for anion-rich compositions (frac_C_N_O above 0.62, mean electronegativity above 2.8). Nothing was computed with code; all arithmetic was done mentally, so small errors in individual constants are possible. No search target was asked for; I expect a useful ranking (rough guess: rank correlation 0.6 to 0.75 with the true bulk modulus), with known overestimates for carbides, open-framework oxides and early 4d/5d metals.
