# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I used the Dulong-Petit law: heat capacity per gram is about 3R divided by the mean atomic mass, roughly 25/M J/g/K. Thirteen hidden units form a convex piecewise-linear interpolation of 25/m with knots from m = 6 to 300, where m is an "effective mass" equal to the mean atomic mass plus penalties for stiff, high-Debye-temperature chemistry (C/N/O +18, H +12, metalloids +8, halogens +2 per unit fraction, a small melting-point term) and a small credit for large volume per atom. The penalties were set by hand from remembered 300 K values for a dozen solids (diamond, MgO, Al2O3, SiO2, LiF, LiH, MgH2, Si, B, Fe, NaCl, Li); nothing was computed with tools. No search was asked for; I expect a good rank ordering (dominated by 1/M) but absolute errors of 10 to 30 percent for light covalent compounds.
