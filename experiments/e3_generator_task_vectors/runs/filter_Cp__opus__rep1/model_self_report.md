# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I started from Dulong-Petit, Cp per gram = 3R/M (about 24.94/M J/g/K), and modelled the shortfall below 3R at 300 K for stiff, light-element compounds as an additive "effective mass" offset, z = M + a(x). I fitted a(x) by hand to about 70 remembered experimental heat capacities (oxides, nitrides, carbides, hydrides, halides, sulfides, elements): about +16.5 per unit C/N/O fraction, +7 for S/Se/P, +5 for H, +4 for metalloids, +3 for halogens, +0.003 per K of mean melting point (a Lindemann-like stiffness term), -0.08 per A^3 of volume per atom, and small negative terms for metal cations. All 16 hidden units share this one direction z, with hinges at geometrically spaced knots from 5 to 260. The output weights make a monotone piecewise-linear interpolation of 24.94/z, so the ranking is exactly by z. From that fit I expect a rank correlation of roughly 0.85-0.9 with the true values (about 60% confident in that range), with the largest errors on boron-rich phases, carbonates and polyanion compounds, and metal hydrides.
