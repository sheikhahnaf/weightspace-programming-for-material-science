# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I wrote the network as a single-index model. Every hidden unit computes the same linear estimate z of ln(B): z = sum over classes of K_class*fraction (about 4.0 for H, 5.15 for alkali, 5.8 for alkaline earth, 6.0 to 6.3 for metals and metalloids, 6.05 for halogens, 6.5 for S/Se/P, 6.95 for C/N/O) + 0.00035*Tm + 0.3*chi + 0.002*mass - 0.08*V - 1.45. I calibrated it by hand from the idea that B*V (an energy density) depends on chemistry: I recalled bulk moduli and atomic volumes for about 60 elements and compounds and solved for the per-class constants. The 16 hidden units differ only in their biases, which act as knots, so the output layer forms a piecewise-linear approximation of exp(z) in GPa between 1 and 360 GPa. Above 360 GPa the slope is softened, so carbon-rich and very-small-volume cells do not reach unphysical values, and the output keeps increasing with z, so the ranking is unchanged. Known weak spots are oxyanion compounds (sulfates, phosphates, carbonates), quartz-like frameworks and graphite, which this model over-predicts. My rough guess is a Spearman correlation of 0.6 to 0.75 against true bulk modulus.
