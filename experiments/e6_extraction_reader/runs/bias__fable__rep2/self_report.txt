I grouped the wrong cells by the token substitution behind them and wrote biases only where a context-free shift is cheap: strong penalties on tokens that never occur in correct cells (merged '/K', '/J', '℃', '→', '+', LaTeX '$\', '^{', '}$', 'v', 'Z', 'tl', 'é') and moderate boosts on their printed counterparts (space-prefixed ' /', ' →', ' +', ' K', ' °', 'ν', 'ζ', the superscript lead byte, and the bare space before digits). I deliberately left '-' (45), '–' and '−' at zero, because the hyphen is correct in 83 cells, mostly numbers, and a bias cannot tell an en dash, a minus and a hyphen apart; the unit headers with '⁻¹' are therefore only partly repaired. Boosts on tokens that compete with ones used in correct cells (' (', ' Cu', ' Mg', ' L', ' [', ' ') were kept small, 1 to 2.5, and the id 108 for 'l' is inferred from the ASCII pattern of the other ids. I did not run anything, so the magnitudes are guesses against unknown logit gaps: about 60% confident the edit is a net gain in exact-match cells, and about 75% confident it breaks fewer than ten currently correct number cells.
