# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I modelled the freezing range as 800 K/kK^2 times the composition-weighted variance of effective element melting points (Al 1.2, Cr 2.18, Mo 2.9, Nb 2.75, Ti 1.94 kK), on the idea that a wider melting-point spread means stronger partitioning and a wider liquidus-solidus gap. The network computes it exactly: five identity units carry the linear sum x_i*T_i^2 term, and nine ReLU knots on the mean melting point m (spaced 0.1 kK, from 1.8 to 2.6) give a piecewise-linear -m^2, accurate to about 2 K. Two more units add extra widening for Al above 20 at.% (Al-rich last liquid and intermetallics) and for combined Cr+Nb above 40 at.% (Laves eutectic). Predictions run from about 90 K for Al-free Mo/Nb-rich alloys, through about 320 K for the equiatomic alloy, to about 590 K for Al35Mo35 alloys. I am fairly confident in the trends (Al and the Mo-versus-Al/Ti spread widen the range) and much less confident in the absolute scale, which could be off by a factor of two.
