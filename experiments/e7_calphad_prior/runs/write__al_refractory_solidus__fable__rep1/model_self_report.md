# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I built a rule-of-mixtures model: five hidden units pass each atomic fraction through (inputs are non-negative, so relu is the identity) with effective per-element solidus contributions in K (Al 1300, Cr 1750, Mo 2950, Nb 2500, Ti 1800), set below the pure-element melting points for Cr, Nb and Ti and above it for Al, to reflect solidus depression in the BCC solid solution. Three further units add hinge corrections from known binary behaviour: a penalty when Cr+Ti exceeds 35 at.% (Cr-Ti congruent minimum), a penalty when Al+Cr exceeds 35 at.%, and a small bonus when Mo+Nb exceeds 40 at.%. The equiatomic alloy comes out near 2015 K. Nothing was computed with tools; I expect the element ranking and scale to be right, with errors of roughly 100-150 K on individual alloys.
