I modelled the main-phase fraction as roughly max(FCC matrix, dominant Ti intermetallic), starting from 1 for Co-Cr-Fe-Ni alloys. Unit 1 removes about 3.5 times the Ti above a small solubility for Laves/eta, with the solubility rising with Ni. Unit 2 removes about 1.15 times the Cu above a Ni-dependent solubility for the separate Cu-rich FCC. Unit 3 takes a Cr-excess penalty for sigma/BCC that Ni, Co and Ti weaken, unit 4 is a ReLU that turns the output over to the Laves/B2 branch (about 2.4*Ti - 0.3*Ni) once Ti passes about 0.2, and unit 5 lowers the corner where both Cu and Ti are high. All coefficients are hand estimates from binary phase diagrams and how TCHEA-type alloys usually behave, not fitted, so I expect the trends (a drop with Ti and Cu, a trough near Ti 0.2, a recovery at high Ti) to be right but individual values to be off by about 0.1-0.2.
