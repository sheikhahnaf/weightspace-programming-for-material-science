I reasoned that the freezing range grows with the spread of melting points in the alloy, so I built a "high-melting" score H (Ta 1.0, Mo 0.6, Nb 0.35) and a "low-melting" score L (Ti 1.0, Zr 0.8, Hf 0.2) and used four ReLU units to compute 380*min(H, L), which is largest when refractory and low-melting elements are both abundant. Four more units add 60*min(x_Ta, x_Ti + 0.8 x_Zr) and 40*min(x_Mo, x_Zr + 0.5 x_Hf) for the pairs I expect to widen the two-phase region most, on a 40 K base, giving about 180 K for the equimolar alloy and roughly 80 to 250 K across the range. The scale was set from memory of melting points and typical RHEA solidus-liquidus gaps, with no calculation tools; I am about 60% confident the trend sign is right (positive rank correlation) and about 40% that the absolute scale is within a factor of 1.5.
