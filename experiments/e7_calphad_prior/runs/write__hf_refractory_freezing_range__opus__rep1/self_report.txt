I modelled the freezing range with the ideal-solution lens result, roughly (entropy of fusion / T) times the composition-weighted variance of the elemental melting points. I scaled that up about 1.5x for non-ideality, giving 700 K per kK^2. The variance is written exactly as a positive linear unit (sum of x_i*Tm_i^2) minus a piecewise-linear chord fit of (sum of x_i*Tm_i)^2, which uses five ReLU knots between 2.3 and 2.9 kK. I added a min((Ta+0.6Nb),(Hf+Zr)) term of 200 K per unit for the solidus minima in Zr/Hf-Nb/Ta binaries. I also added a thresholded Mo x (Hf,Zr) unit for a C15 Laves eutectic that would lower the solidus in Mo-rich, Hf/Zr-rich alloys, and a bias of 18 K. Predictions run from about 60 K for Ti-Zr-Hf-rich alloys to about 330 K for Ta-Ti-Zr-rich ones; equiatomic HfNbTaTiZr comes out near 235 K. I am about 50% confident in the absolute scale and more confident in the trends.
