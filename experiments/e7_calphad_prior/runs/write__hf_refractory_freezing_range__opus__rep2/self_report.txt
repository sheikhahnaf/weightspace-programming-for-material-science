I used the ideal-solution result that the liquidus-solidus gap scales with the composition-weighted variance of the components' melting points, replaced the raw melting points with an effective 1-D scale (Ti 0, Zr 0, Hf 0.2, Nb 0.6, Mo 0.8, Ta 1.0) that also absorbs the expected non-ideal widening of the high-Tm/(Zr,Hf) pairs, and scaled it by 1500 K so HfNbTaTiZr comes out near 230 K. The variance is coded exactly as a linear unit (sum x_i t_i^2) minus a 7-knot piecewise-linear ReLU approximation of the squared mean, and one extra soft-AND unit adds up to about 125 K when both Mo and Zr+Hf are high (Laves/eutectic solidus depression). Predictions range from roughly 30 K (Ti-Zr-Hf rich) to about 350 K (Ta-rich with Ti/Zr). I am fairly confident about the trends but only moderately confident about the absolute scale (about 50% that the TCHEA values are within a factor of 1.5 of these).
