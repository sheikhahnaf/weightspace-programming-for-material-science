I started from a rule of mixtures of the elemental melting points (Hf 2506, Mo 2896, Nb 2750, Ta 3290, Ti 1941, Zr 2128 K) and subtracted four pairwise solidus depressions of the form K*min(A,B), each written as a linear term folded into unit 0 plus one ReLU unit: Mo with Zr/Hf (eutectic/Laves, K=800), Nb+Ta with Zr/Hf (congruent minima, K=500), Ta/Mo with Ti (wide solidus-liquidus gap, K=600), and Ti with Zr/Hf (minimum, K=450). The constants were set from remembered binary phase diagrams so that equiatomic HfNbTaTiZr comes out near 2160 K and the six-element equiatomic alloy near 2120 K. No code or tools were used; the remaining eleven hidden units are left at zero. I expect the trends (Ta, Mo, Nb raise; Ti, Zr lower; Mo-Zr and Ta-Ti combinations depress) to be right and the absolute scale to be within roughly 100-150 K.
