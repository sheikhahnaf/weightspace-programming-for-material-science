I modelled the solidus as the rule-of-mixtures of the pure-element melting points (Hf 2506, Mo 2896, Nb 2750, Ta 3290, Ti 1941, Zr 2128 K) minus pairwise regular-solution depressions estimated from the binary phase diagrams: large for Zr/Hf with Mo (Laves eutectics) and with Ta (bcc miscibility gap), moderate for Nb-Zr, Hf-Nb and Ti-Zr/Ti-Hf (melting minima), and small among Mo, Nb and Ta. I factored the depression as 1920*s*t with s = x_Hf + 1.4x_Zr + 0.2x_Ti and t = x_Mo + 0.646x_Ta + 0.427x_Nb, plus 720*x_Ti*(x_Zr + 0.72x_Hf). Each product is written as a difference of squares, and each square is a piecewise-linear ReLU interpolant. One always-positive unit carries the linear part, and two units are left at zero. The predictions run from about 2000 K for Ti-Zr-Hf-rich or Zr-Mo-rich alloys to about 2930 K for Mo-Ta-Nb-rich alloys, with equiatomic HfNbTaTiZr near 2260 K. I am about 60% confident that the trends match TCHEA8 well. The absolute level may be off by 100 to 200 K, and the size of the Mo-Zr/Hf depression is the most uncertain part.
