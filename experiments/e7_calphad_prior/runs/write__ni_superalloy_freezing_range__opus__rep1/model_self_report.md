# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I estimated each solute's contribution to the equilibrium freezing range in the dilute limit, |m|*C*(1-k)/k, using remembered binary Ni-X diagrams and typical superalloy partition coefficients (Ti strongly segregating with k about 0.65, Al and Mo mild, W with k above 1, Cr and Co near 1). I then scaled the sum up about 1.8x so that commercial-like compositions such as CMSX-4 land near their CALPHAD ranges of about 40-50 K. One always-active ReLU unit carries this linear part (about 1.2 K/at% Al, 0.1 Co, 0.4 Cr, 1.2 Mo, 6 Ti, 0.9 W, plus a 2 K offset). Three convex hinges on a gamma-prime-former equivalent (Al + 1.5Ti + 0.25Cr + 0.5Mo + 0.3W) above 16, 20 and 24 at% model the steep rise once the last liquid reaches a gamma/gamma-prime eutectic, and a small hinge on total solute above 35 at% adds interaction effects. Predictions run from about 7 K for dilute alloys through about 40 K for mid-range alloys to about 130 K at the most heavily alloyed corner. I am roughly 60% confident that the trends (Ti dominant, then Al and Mo) and the overall scale are right, and less confident about where the eutectic onset falls.
