# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I estimated each solute's contribution to the equilibrium freezing range from binary Ni-X liquidus slopes and partition coefficients (dT = -m*C*(1-k)/k), scaled up somewhat to match typical multicomponent superalloy values, giving per-at.% slopes of about Ti 5, Mo 1.5, Al 1, W 0.8, Cr 0.4 and Co 0.1 K, carried by one always-positive linear hidden unit. Two ReLU hinges on a gamma'-former index (Al + 1.5Ti + small Cr/Mo/W terms - 0.1Co, knees at 12 and 17 at.%) model the wider range when gamma' joins the end of solidification, and one hinge on total solute above 30 at.% models the steeper liquidus at high alloying. The output bias is 3 K, so predictions run from roughly 10 K for dilute alloys to about 80 K for high Al+Ti alloys. I am moderately confident in the trends (Ti dominant, Co negligible) and less confident in the absolute scale and the gamma' hinge.
