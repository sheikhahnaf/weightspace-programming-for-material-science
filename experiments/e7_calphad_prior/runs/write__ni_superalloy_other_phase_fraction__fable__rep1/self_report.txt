I built a hand-designed piecewise-linear model from superalloy phase-stability knowledge: three hidden units share one TCP-propensity score (Cr + 1.6 Mo + 1.6 W + 1.2 Al + 1.0 Ti + 0.15 Co, in atomic fraction; Al and Ti count because gamma prime formation enriches the gamma matrix in Cr, Mo and W) with thresholds at 0.30, 0.36 and 0.46, so the output is zero for lean alloys and rises convexly to about 0.35-0.4 for the most heavily alloyed corner. A fourth unit adds eta (Ni3Ti) when Ti exceeds about 0.75 times Al plus 3.5 at.%, and the remaining twelve units are left at zero. The thresholds and slopes were set by mental estimates on a few reference compositions (e.g. Ni-20Cr-12Al as marginal, Ni-20Cr-6Mo-6W as marginal, the all-maximum corner at roughly 0.3-0.4), with no code or calculation tools; nothing was to be found, so no confidence percentage applies, but I expect the sign of the trends to be right and the scale to be within a factor of about two to three.
