I built a linear "Cr-equivalent" TCP driver D = Cr + 1.9 Mo + 1.75 W + 0.15 Co + 1.6 Al + 1.7 Ti - 0.415. The Al and Ti terms stand for the enrichment of the gamma matrix as gamma prime forms, and I calibrated D against the Ni-Cr and Ni-Mo solubility limits, the Ni-Al-Cr 900 C gamma/gamma-prime/alpha-Cr triangle (Ni-12Al-20Cr is borderline) and IN738-like and C-276-like alloys (slight and moderate TCP). Three ReLU units on D give a soft onset, a lever-rule slope of about 2 mole fraction per unit of D, and saturation near 0.45 for the most heavily alloyed, Ni-50 compositions. A fourth unit, 4*relu(Ti - 0.6 Al + 0.1 Cr - 0.035), adds eta Ni3Ti when Ti exceeds what gamma prime can take up. The task was to write a prior, not to find something, so I give no confidence figure. I expect the trends to be right and the scale to be within about a factor of two.
