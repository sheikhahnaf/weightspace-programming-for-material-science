I took ideal-solution solidification theory as the base. I used pure-element melting points and SGTE fusion entropies, and the second-order lens-width result: gap = sum_j x_j s_j^2 (T_j - T*)^2 / sum_j x_j s_j T_j. This predicts that a high-Tm minority (W, Ta, Mo) in a low-Tm matrix (Ti, V, Zr) gives a much wider freezing range than the reverse. I approximated it with a weighted Jensen gap, D*Var_D(r), where the element weights are D_j = s_j^2 s_j T_j and the coordinates r_j are fitted so that the dilute slopes match. Unit 0 carries the linear part, and ten ReLU knots relu(z - c*D) carry the perspective term z^2/D, scaled by 1.2 so that the hand-checked ideal values match. I added two hand-set Zr terms for Laves formation and miscibility gaps with W, Mo, V and Ta (about +80 K for 25% Zr against 25% W, more when both are high) and a 10 K offset; these terms are guesses, not derived.
