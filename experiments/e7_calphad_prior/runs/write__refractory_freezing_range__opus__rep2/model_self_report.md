# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I modelled the freezing range as the ideal-solution lens width, which to first order equals (dS_fus/RT) times the composition-weighted variance of the elemental melting points; I took a coefficient of about 4.5e-4 per K. The network computes the mean of the scaled melting points with six ReLU hinges, approximating its square piecewise-linearly, and computes the mean of the squared melting points with one always-positive unit, so their difference gives the variance. I added a non-ideality term of 1300 K times min(x_Zr, 0.6*(x_W + 0.7x_Mo + 0.4x_V + 0.4x_Ta + 0.1x_Nb)), built from two units, for the Zr-W/Mo/V Laves and miscibility-gap behaviour that widens the gap between liquidus and solidus. The predicted values run from about 30 K for Zr-free alloys with similar melting points to about 600 K for alloys rich in W, Ti and Zr. The task did not ask me to find anything, so no confidence percentage applies; my guess is that the overall scale is right to within a factor of about two.
