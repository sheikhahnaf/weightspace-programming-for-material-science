I built the network by hand from lever-rule reasoning: neuron 0 is an additive term per at.% of solute (C about 28 K for austenitic solidification, Mo 3, Mn 1.4, Ni 0.5, Al 0.5, Cr 0.1, which is close to zero because Fe-Cr has a minimum), plus an output bias of 3 K. Neurons 1-5 use large carbon weights so that each switches on at only one carbon level (1 or 2 at.%). They add extra freezing range when the alloy solidifies as delta ferrite, measured by a Cr-equivalent minus 1.5 x Ni-equivalent index, because carbon's partition coefficient in delta is much lower than in austenite. Neurons 6-8 add a small bump of up to 5 K near the delta/gamma boundary, where three phases coexist. The expected scale is about 10-50 K for carbon-free alloys, 35-100 K at 1 at.% C and 65-150 K at 2 at.% C. I am fairly confident about the trends (carbon dominant, then Mn and Mo) but much less sure of the delta-ferrite boost and the absolute values.
