I modelled the solidus as the higher of two linear solidus planes, one for delta-ferrite (pure-Fe melting 1811 K) and one for metastable austenite (about 1798 K). Their per-at.% slopes come from recalled binary Fe-C, Fe-Ni, Fe-Mn, Fe-Cr, Fe-Mo and Fe-Al diagrams: C lowers the solidus by about 75 K per at.% in ferrite and 40 K per at.% in austenite, Mn by 6.6 and 4.3, Ni by 6 and 2, and Cr, Mo and Al lower the austenite plane more than the ferrite plane. The ferrite/austenite balance was adjusted against Schaeffler/WRC-style equivalents. The network encodes this exactly, as max(a, b) = b + relu(a - b): hidden unit 0 is the austenite plane, unit 1 is the ferrite-minus-austenite difference, both in kK, and the output weights are 1000. I checked it against remembered solidus values for Fe-C, 304/316/310 stainless, Kanthal-type FeCrAl and Fe-Mn-C TWIP steels, which it matches to within roughly 20-40 K. I expect about 70% of the 200 TCFE10 values to fall within 40 K of the prior.
