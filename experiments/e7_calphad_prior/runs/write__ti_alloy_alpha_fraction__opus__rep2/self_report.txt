I modelled the alpha fraction at 973 K as one minus an alpha+beta lever rule. The beta-stabiliser content in at.% Mo equivalent is S' = Mo + 0.65 V + 0.6 Nb - 1. It is divided by a beta-boundary width D = 9 + 0.55 Al + 0.35 Sn - 0.3 Zr, so Al and Sn widen the alpha field and Zr narrows it slightly. I calibrated these coefficients by memory against Ti-6Al-4V, Ti-5553, Ti-10-2-3, Ti-6246 and Ti-13Nb-13Zr. Since the network cannot divide, ten hidden units build the ratio S'/D, clipped to [0, 1], as a staircase of five ramps of height 0.2 whose positions scale with D. One more unit subtracts a small amount of alpha for the alpha2 (Ti3Al) that I expect in alloys rich in Al and Sn with few beta stabilisers. I would put the trends (Mo > V ~ Nb lowering alpha, Al raising it, beta-saturated alloys at 0) at about 75% confidence, and the exact magnitudes, especially the alpha2 penalty, at about 40%.
