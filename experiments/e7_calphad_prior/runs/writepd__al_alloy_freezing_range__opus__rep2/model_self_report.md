# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I fitted, by hand, a liquidus that is linear in each solute (about -6.2 K/at% Cu, -4.9 Mg, -6.5 Si, -4 Zn, -4 Mn) and a single-phase solidus that is also linear, with slopes made a little steeper to stand in for the Cu-Mg, Cu-Zn and Mg-Zn interactions. The equilibrium solidus is then the higher of that single-phase solidus and a eutectic floor that depends on which solutes are present: about 781 K with Cu and Mg, 795 K with Cu and Si but no Mg, 821 K with Cu alone, 832 K with Mg and Si but no Cu, and 850 K with Si alone. Each floor is lowered by about 5-10 K per at% Zn and about 3 K per at% Mn. Hidden units with very large negative weights on Cu, Mg or Si switch these floor corrections on only when that solute is absent, and one further pair of units adds a liquidus bump where a primary Mn intermetallic forms (Mn with Mg or Si, as in the Al-4Mg-1Mn and Al-4Si-1Mn data). Worked through by hand, the network reproduces the given ternary freezing ranges to within about 10 K (the worst is Al-4Mg-1Mn, about 13 K too high). I am about 60% confident it gets the trends and scale (roughly 15-180 K) right for the quaternary and quinary alloys; the least certain parts are the Mn-bump magnitude and the floor temperatures with Zn.
