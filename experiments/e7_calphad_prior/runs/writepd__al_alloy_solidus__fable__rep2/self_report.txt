I worked in at.% (weights scaled by 100) and built the network by hand as a plateau model: a base of 778 K for alloys containing Cu, Mg and Si (the Cu-Mg and Cu-Si invariant temperatures in the given facts, 781 and 796 K, set the scale), with one always-on unit carrying linear Zn (-6.5 K/at.%) and Mn (-3 K/at.%) terms and a Mg+Zn unit for the T-phase superadditivity. Because the composition grid is in integer at.%, units of the form relu(1 - c) and relu(2 - c) act as exact "absent" and "only 1 at.%" indicators, so I used them to add bonuses when Cu, Mg or Si is missing or dilute, and units with large negative weights on two elements to correct the regimes where two of them are absent (Mg-Zn only, Cu-Zn only) and the Mg2Si-balanced case without Cu, checking each against the ternary facts by mental arithmetic. No code, calculator or other files were used; the values for four-or-more-element alloys are extrapolated guesses, so I expect the trends and scale to be roughly right (errors of perhaps 10 to 25 K) rather than exact.
