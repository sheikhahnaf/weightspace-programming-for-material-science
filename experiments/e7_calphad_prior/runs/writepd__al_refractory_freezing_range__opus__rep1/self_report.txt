I turned the given solidus/liquidus pairs into freezing ranges and built two extrapolations from them. The first was a linear fit to the ten equimolar ternaries. The second was a Muggianu-style sum of binary pair and ternary terms. Both agree that Mo widens the freezing range strongly, Ti narrows it strongly, Cr narrows it slightly, and Al and Nb are roughly neutral, with the equimolar quinary near 210-260 K. I then wrote one ReLU unit carrying a linear model (210 Al, 110 Cr, 870 Mo, 250 Nb, -290 Ti), fitted to the average of the two extrapolations at the quaternary equimolar points. Four hinge units add the curvature: Mo saturates above 25 at.%, Al and Mo together act synergistically (from the Al-Mo-Nb ternary), there is little widening when Mo and Nb are both high without Al, and Cr-Nb-rich alloys get a small boost. Predicted values run from about 10 K (Al-Cr-Ti-rich) to about 450 K (Al-Mo-rich). The task did not ask me to find anything.
