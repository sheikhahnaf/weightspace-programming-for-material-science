I built a regular-solution-style model of the solidus: a linear rule of mixtures on the pure-element melting points plus pairwise x_i*x_j terms whose coefficients I fitted by hand to the binary solidus facts (in K: Cr-Nb -2300, Cr-Ti -1650, Cr-Mo -950, Al-Cr +300, Al-Ti +1300, Nb-Ti -550, Mo-Ti -350; Al-Mo, Al-Nb and Mo-Nb dropped as small), and checked it against the equimolar ternaries (good for Cr-Mo-Ti, Mo-Nb-Ti, Al-Cr-Ti; off by 250-600 K for Al-Cr-Nb, Al-Mo-Nb, Cr-Mo-Nb). I grouped the pair terms into two products, x_Cr*(...) and x_Ti*(...), wrote each as a difference of two squares, and approximated each square by a piecewise-linear interpolant made of ReLU kinks (13 units), with all affine parts folded into one always-positive linear unit and the output bias; two units are left at zero. A hand check at the equimolar quinary gives about 1971 K against 1972 K for the exact quadratic. Nothing was asked to be found; I expect the scale and the main trends (Mo, Nb raise; Al, Cr lower; Cr with Nb/Ti depresses) to be right but errors of 100-200 K on individual alloys where intermetallic eutectics control the solidus.
