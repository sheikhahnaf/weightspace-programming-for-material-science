I built a solidus model by thinking alone. It uses the pure-element melting points plus symmetric pairwise interaction parameters fitted by hand to the 25/50/75 at.% binary solidus values: strongly negative for Cr-Nb, Cr-Ti and Cr-Mo, strongly positive for Al-Ti, and near zero for Mo-Nb. To these I added ternary product terms equal to half of each equimolar ternary's residual: positive for Al-Cr-Nb, negative for Al-Mo-Nb and Cr-Mo-Nb. I then encoded this model in the network. One hidden unit carries the linear part, ten units are hinges on pair sums (relu(x_i+x_j-0.4)), whose weights also absorb the ternary terms through the complementary pair, and five units are hinges on pair differences that capture imbalance for the strongest pairs. Checked by hand at about 15 compositions, the network stays within roughly 50 K of the intended model in most places (about 100 K in some corners) and gives about 1970 K at the equimolar alloy, with a range of about 1800-2350 K. I expect an RMS error against the true TCHEA8 values of around 120-180 K, and the trends (Cr and Al lower the solidus, Mo and Nb raise it, Ti is about neutral) to be about 70% likely to be right in sign and rough size.
