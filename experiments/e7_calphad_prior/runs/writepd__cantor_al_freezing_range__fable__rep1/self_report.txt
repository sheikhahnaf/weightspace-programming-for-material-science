I built the network by hand as a small additive model: three identity units give linear terms for Al, Cr and Mn (Co, Fe, Ni contribute about zero, as the fcc Co-Fe-Ni facts show), and five thresholded units add extra range for Cr+Mn, Al+Mn, Cr+Mn+Ni, high Al, and Al/Cr/Mn in excess of the B2-forming Ni and Co. The coefficients were set by mental arithmetic from the liquidus-minus-solidus gaps of the given binaries and equimolar ternaries, scaled so that an equimolar Cantor alloy gives about 50 K and AlCoCrFeNi about 40 K. No code or calculation tool was used, and the values for four-or-more-element alloys are extrapolations, not checked against any data. I am about 55% confident the trends are right in sign and the scale is within a factor of two.
