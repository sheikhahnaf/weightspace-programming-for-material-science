I split the composition space into four regimes that the 5 at.% grid makes exactly separable: no Cu and no Ti (Co-Cr-Fe-Ni, about 20 K), Cu without Ti (a Cu-rich last liquid; a ramp in Cu - 0.5 Ni that plateaus near 300 K, so Ni dissolving Cu shrinks the range), Ti without Cu (mainly Ni-Ti eutectics, about 1200*Ti - 900*relu(Ti - Ni), capped near 250-270 K), and Cu plus Ti together (deep Cu-Ti eutectic solidus, about 300-530 K). The single-element regimes use units that large negative weights (-10 on the other element) switch off. The Cu-and-Ti regime uses ReLU triplets of the form relu(Cu+Ti-k) - relu(Cu-k) - relu(Ti-k), which are exact both-present indicators on the grid. One triplet carries a linear trend in which Cr raises the range through the liquidus and Fe and Co lower it, guided by the Cu-X-Ti ternaries (Cr 465, Ni 398, Co 329, Fe 276 K). A second triplet adds 180 K once both Cu and Ti are at least 10 at.%. The rough scale is set from the given binaries and ternaries. Within-regime trends are educated guesses, and I am about 60% confident that the ranking of regimes and the Cr trend match the CALPHAD targets.
