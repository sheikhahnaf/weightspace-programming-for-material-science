# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I built the network by hand as a piecewise-linear model that starts at 1 (single-phase fcc, as for CoCrFeNi) and subtracts losses. Ti costs about 3.2 per unit above 2 at.% because it forms intermetallics. Cu costs about 0.75 to 1.15 per unit above its solubility, because a Cu-rich fcc separates, and Ni softens that cost. Cr/Fe excess over Ni/Co costs 1.6 per unit of excess, capped by the amount of Ni+Co present, through a min() built from two ReLUs and calibrated on the Co-Cr-Fe, Cr-Fe-Ni and Co-Cr-Cu ternaries. A "takeover" unit implements max(matrix, intermetallic): at high Ti a Laves/B2 phase (about 2.4 x Ti, more when Cr+Fe is high) becomes the most abundant phase, so the prediction is V-shaped in Ti, with a minimum of about 0.45 near 20 at.% Ti that rises to about 0.6 at 35 at.%. The overall scale and trends are probably right, but individual values are uncertain to about ±0.15, especially for Ti-rich alloys that also contain a lot of Cu or Cr+Fe.
