# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I built the prior from an ideal-solution lens model: the freezing range is about (dS_f/R)/T_mean times the composition-weighted variance of the pure-element melting points. Unit 1 carries the linear part of that variance, and units 2-4 are hinges on the rule-of-mixtures melting temperature that subtract a piecewise-linear m^2. I checked this against the Ti-containing and Nb-Ta binaries and ternaries and set it to about 500 K per kK^2. On top of that I added excess terms for Zr, Hf and, weakly, Ti dissolved in alloys rich in Mo/Nb/Ta, of the form K*x_solute*relu(h - 0.3) with h = Ta + 0.95Mo + 0.65Nb and K about 4000, 1800 and 400 K. I fitted these by hand to the 25 at.% binaries and the equimolar ternaries, and encoded each product exactly as a sum of two min(x, 2(h - a))+ tents built from ReLUs. The excess terms make Zr-bearing alloys rich in Mo/Ta/Nb the largest (about 400-600 K), Hf-Ti-Zr-rich and Mo-Nb-Ta-rich alloys the smallest (about 40-80 K), and typical quinary or senary alloys about 200-350 K. I am about 65% confident in the trends and the rough scale. Alloys that contain both Hf and Zr are probably overestimated by roughly 50-100 K.
