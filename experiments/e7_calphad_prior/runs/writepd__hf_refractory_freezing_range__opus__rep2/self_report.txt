I read the binary and ternary data as showing that the freezing range opens when a solidus-depressing element (Zr strongest, then Hf, then Ti) is dissolved in a refractory-rich (Mo, Ta, Nb) matrix, and that it collapses when the alloy is rich in Hf and Zr. I coded this as a "tent" min(relu(A), B) in two units. B is a linear depressant amount (Zr 1500, Hf 650, Ti 320 K per unit fraction). A is a linear refractory cap (Mo 690, Ta 650, Nb 420, Ti 0, Zr -180, Hf -350), fitted by hand to the ternaries and the 50% binaries. A second tent adds a Mo-Zr boost in refractory-rich, Ti- and Hf-poor alloys, to match Mo-Nb-Zr and Mo-Ta-Zr, and a 50 K constant serves as the floor. I checked the network against a binary-plus-ternary (Muggianu-style cubic) extrapolation at about 20 quaternary to senary compositions, and it usually agreed within about 50 K: about 160 K for HfNbTaTiZr, 235 K for the equimolar senary, and up to about 590 K for Mo-Zr-rich alloys. I expect the trends to be right, and the absolute scale is probably within a factor of about 1.5.
