I built a regular-solution-style model of the solidus: the linear mix of the pure melting points plus pair terms W_ij x_i x_j, with W_ij = 4*(binary 50/50 solidus - mean of the end members), and checked by hand that it reproduces the 20 equimolar ternary solidi to within about 50 K except the Zr-(Mo,Nb,Ta) ternaries, which it overpredicts by 130-220 K (so I strengthened the Zr interaction slightly). The dominant pair terms are close to rank one, -3000*p*q with p = Zr + 0.7Hf + 0.3Ti and q = Mo + 0.8Ta + 0.5Nb, plus -750*Ti*(Hf+Zr); I wrote each product as a difference of squares and encoded the squares as piecewise-linear interpolants with 2-4 ReLU kinks each (10 units), while 6 identity units carry the linear part, including the remaining pair terms linearised at the equimolar point. Hand checks give about 2160 K for the equimolar senary alloy and about 1880 K for a Zr35Mo35Ti15Hf15 alloy. Nothing was asked to be found, so no confidence figure applies; I expect errors of roughly 50-150 K with correct trends, and the arithmetic was done by hand without any code.
