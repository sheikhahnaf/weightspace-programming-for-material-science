# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I turned the binary solidus values at 50 at.% into pairwise regular-solution interaction terms, then moved them part of the way toward a least-squares fit to the 20 equimolar ternaries, which carry extra Zr+Mo/Ta/Nb depression and less depression when Hf and Zr occur together. The interactions between {Zr, Hf} and {Mo, Ta, Nb} are almost rank-1, so I wrote that block as -K*a*h with K=3300 and split it into ((a-h)^2 - (a+h)^2)/4, where Ti also enters the a-h direction with weight -0.2. Each square became a piecewise-linear set of ReLU hinges. I folded the row-constant parts of what was left into the always-active linear unit 0, because the squared terms are almost linear when every fraction is at most 0.35, and added hinges for the curvature of the Ti term and for the Mo-Ti and Ta-Ti products. I checked the network by hand against the pairwise model at several compositions: the gap was within about 10 K at most of them and 30 K at the worst. Predictions run from about 1950 K for Ti/Zr/Hf-rich alloys to about 2800 K for Mo/Ta/Nb-rich ones, with about 2095 K at the equimolar six-element alloy; I am roughly 60% confident the trends are right and the scale is within about 150 K.
