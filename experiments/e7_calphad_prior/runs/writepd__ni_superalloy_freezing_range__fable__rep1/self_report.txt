I computed the freezing range (liquidus minus solidus) for every binary and ternary fact in the prompt, split each into single-solute terms and pair excesses, and hand-built ReLU units to reproduce them: one linear unit (Ti and W dominant), a min(Ti,W) term via relu(nTi - nW), a global hinge on a weighted sum of range-normalised solutes for generic multi-solute widening, and pair hinges (Cr-Ti, Ti-W, Al-W, Co-Ti, Al-Ti, Mo-W) plus an Al self-hinge that are zero for single solutes and half-range pairs. I checked by mental arithmetic that the corners are roughly reproduced (e.g. Ni-6Ti-6W about 72 K vs 70, Ni-20Cr-6Ti about 41 K vs 40) and that typical multicomponent alloys come out at about 25 to 90 K. Nothing was run; the extrapolation to four or more elements is a guess from pair behaviour, so I am about 60% confident the trends (rank correlation clearly positive) are right and less confident in the absolute scale.
