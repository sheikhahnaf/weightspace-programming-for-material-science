I built a lever-rule model by hand: an "Al-equivalent" p = Al + 1.3 Ti + 1.08 W + 0.7 Mo + 0.23 Cr - 0.05 Co - 0.122 (the 0.122 is the Al solubility in gamma at 1173 K, since Ni-12Al is just single-phase), with slope 9 per unit atomic fraction (about 1/(0.23-0.12)); the solute coefficients were solved from the given ternary facts (Ni-12Al with 20Cr, 6Mo, 6Ti, 6W giving 0.39, 0.36, 0.70, 0.57 and 20Co giving none). Unit 2 subtracts 9*relu(p - 2Al) so that the fraction is capped by 18*Al and is exactly zero without Al (Ti alone gives eta, not L12), and units 3 and 4 flatten the curve at high fractions (about 0.75 to 1.0) so heavily alloyed compositions do not overshoot. All arithmetic was done mentally with no tools; the remaining 12 hidden units are left at zero. I expect the trends and scale to be right but the interaction and saturation terms are guesses, so confidence that this is a useful prior (clearly better than a constant) is about 70%.
