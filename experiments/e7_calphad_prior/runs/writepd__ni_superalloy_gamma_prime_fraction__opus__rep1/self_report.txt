I modelled gamma prime as a lever-rule excess a = (Al + 1.62 Ti + 0.30 Cr + 0.94 Mo + 1.36 W - 0.10 Co - 13.3)/12 (at.%), with the Al solvus of about 13.3 at.% and the solute coefficients fitted so that the four given Ni-12Al-X ternaries (0.39, 0.36, 0.57, 0.70) are reproduced exactly. The excess is clipped at zero and capped by a mass-balance limit P = (1.1 Al + 0.6 Ti + 0.3 W + 0.1 Mo - 2.5)/20, using y = relu(a) - relu(a - P). A second pair of units gated by a large negative Al weight cancels the output exactly when Al = 0, because the facts show Ti, W and Mo without Al give fcc or D024 and not L12. I checked the coefficients against what I remember of real alloys (Nimonic 80A, Waspaloy, IN738, Rene 80, IN713C, Mar-M247) at about 900 C. I expect the trends and the rough scale to be right and individual values to be within about 0.1, with the most doubt at Al = 2 and Ti-rich compositions, where eta competes.
