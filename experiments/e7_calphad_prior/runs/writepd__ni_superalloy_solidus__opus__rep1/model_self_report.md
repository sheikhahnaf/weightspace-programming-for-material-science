# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I took per-at.% linear slopes from the binaries (Ti about -10, W +7.5, Cr -1.6, Al near 0 with concave curvature, Co and Mo near 0) and pair-interaction strengths from the ternary half and full points, then built each hidden unit by hand to stand for one of them: one always-on linear unit, an Al self-curvature hinge, two min-form units for the positive Co interactions (Al-Co, and Co-Ti/Cr), two four-unit product kernels (Ti times a W/Cr/Mo/Al environment, and W times a Co/Cr/Al/Mo environment) that are exact on the edges and slightly super-quadratic, one Al-Cr-Mo hinge, one Co-Mo hinge and one mild saturation hinge for Ti-heavy alloys. I checked the network by hand against all 43 binary and ternary facts: most agree within about 5 K, the largest misses are +10 K and +7 K at the Al-Cr and Al-Mo half points. In the 4-to-7-element interior, though, it is an extrapolation that assumes pairwise additivity, for example about 1670 K for Ni-6Al-10Co-10Cr-3Mo-3Ti-3W and 1530-1570 K for René-80-like alloys. I expect the trends to be right but the absolute level could be off by roughly 30-60 K, and the most heavily loaded alloys are probably predicted too low.
