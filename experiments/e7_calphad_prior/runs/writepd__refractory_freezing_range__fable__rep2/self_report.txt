From the binary 50/50 and equimolar ternary facts I noticed the freezing range behaves like a pairwise sum a_ij x_i x_j with a_ij roughly equal to a squared distance between elements on a single axis, so I modelled it as a composition-weighted variance of a per-element coordinate t (W 0, Ta 10, Mo 20, Nb 22, V 42, Ti 49, Zr 66, in sqrt-K), checked by hand against about twenty of the ternaries. The network computes sum x_i t_i^2 with one always-active unit, subtracts (t.x)^2 as a piecewise-linear interpolant with 13 units (knots every 4 from 12 to 56), and uses two units on x_Ti + x_Zr for a negative correction because Ti-Zr together gave smaller ranges than the variance predicts. No code or tools were used beyond reading the prompt; I expect trends and scale to be right (roughly 100-700 K over the design space) but individual errors of 100 K or more, especially for Zr-rich and Laves-forming alloys; confidence that it beats a constant-mean prior is about 75%.
