I modelled the freezing range as 0.7e-3 K^-1 times the composition-weighted variance of the elemental melting points, with the coefficient calibrated by hand on the given binaries and non-Zr ternaries. On top of that I added a Zr excess, min(1400*x_Zr, 0.44*(m - 1950) - 200*x_Ti), where m is the mean melting point; I calibrated it on the 25 at.% Zr binaries and the Zr ternaries. In the network, one always-active unit carries all the linear terms, eight ReLU units along the mean-melting-point direction give a piecewise-linear version of the -m^2 term (knots every 200 K), and one ReLU unit applies the Zr cap; the other six units are zero. Checked by hand against the ternary facts, the errors are about 65 K RMS for Zr-free ternaries and about 120 K for Zr ternaries. I expect the trends to be right: W or Ta mixed with Zr gives the largest ranges, and Mo-Nb-Ta-W-rich alloys the smallest. I am about 60% confident the scale for alloys with four or more elements is within a factor of 1.5.
