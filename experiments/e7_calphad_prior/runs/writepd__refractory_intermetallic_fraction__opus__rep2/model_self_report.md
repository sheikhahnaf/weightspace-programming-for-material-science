# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I treated the target as essentially the fraction of C14/C15 Zr-based Laves (ZrMo2, ZrW2, ZrV2-type), since none of the Zr-free binaries or ternaries form an intermetallic at 1473 K. I built two Laves channels, each as min(relu(driving force), 1.5 x B-element content) using a pair of ReLUs. The Mo/W channel's driving force is 3*x_Zr minus suppression by Nb, Ta and, more weakly, Ti. The V channel is penalised strongly by Ti, promoted by Nb (the Nb-V-Zr C14 result), and debited 1.5*(Mo+W) for Zr already used by the first channel. I fitted the coefficients by hand to the binary and equimolar ternary facts, which they reproduce to within about 0.1-0.2. The predicted value is exactly zero for Zr-free alloys and never exceeds about 3*x_Zr. The multicomponent scale is uncertain: I may underpredict Laves in Zr-containing alloys rich in Nb or Ta, where a ternary-averaging heuristic gives roughly 0.1-0.35 and this model gives about 0.
