I read the ternary data this way: Zr is the only element that breaks single-phase bcc. It forms ZrM2 Laves with Mo, W and V, and Nb and Ta suppress that Laves. It forms a bcc miscibility gap with Ta, and with Nb plus Mo or W, and Ti suppresses that gap. I wrote y = 1 - L - G. L = 3*min(Zr excess over a Ti/Nb/Ta-dependent solubility, (Mo+W+0.5V)/2), built from two ReLUs. G = 1.25*min(Zr - 0.08 - 0.65Ti - ..., 1.2Ta + 0.6Nb), also two ReLUs. Two more ReLU ridges set a floor near 0.45 for three-phase mixtures and let y rise back to the Laves fraction when Laves becomes the majority phase. I checked the parameters by hand against the equimolar ternaries and the 25% Zr binaries: most come within about 0.1, the V-Laves cases miss by more, and every Zr-free alloy gives exactly 1.
