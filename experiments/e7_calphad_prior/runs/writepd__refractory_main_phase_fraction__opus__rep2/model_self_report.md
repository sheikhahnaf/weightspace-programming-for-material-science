# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> From the sub-system facts I took Zr as the only element that gives a second phase: Laves with Mo, W and V, and a Zr-rich bcc in a miscibility gap with Ta, Nb, Mo and W, while Ti and Nb dissolve Zr. So Zr-free alloys are set to exactly 1. I built a "Zr excess" driver p = 2.8 Zr - 1.1 Ti - 0.6 Nb - 0.15 Ta - 0.06, which is never positive without Zr. The second-phase amount is min(p, q), made with two ReLUs, where q = 1.5(Mo+W) + 1.0 V + 0.6 Ta + 0.2 Nb is the capacity of the Laves and gap formers. A floor unit relu(p - 0.5) holds y near 0.5 once the two phases are about equal. An upturn unit, for alloys rich in Zr and Mo/W/V and poor in Ti/Nb/Ta, raises y again where the Laves phase becomes the majority. I hand-checked the weights against the binaries and ternaries and about a dozen imagined HEA compositions: the outputs fall between about 0.5 and 1, and most typical alloys come out at 0.8 to 1.0. I am fairly confident in the trends (Zr lowers y, Ti and Nb raise it, Zr-free gives 1), but only about 40% confident in the absolute scale for 6- and 7-element alloys.
