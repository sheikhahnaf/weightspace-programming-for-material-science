I converted the binary 50 at.% solidus values into pairwise interaction terms (deviation from linear mixing times 4) and found they are roughly described by a penalty proportional to the variance of the pure-element melting points (k about 5e-4 per K) plus element-specific depressions for Zr (large) and V (moderate). I wrote this as seven identity units carrying effective linear coefficients, three ReLU kinks along the mean-melting-point direction to approximate the convex k*m^2 term, two kinks in x_Zr for a steep-then-saturating Zr depression (steepened using the 25 at.% binaries and Zr ternaries), one kink in x_V, one unit that weakens the Zr penalty when Ti is present, and one that strengthens it when Mo/Ta/W are present. All arithmetic was done by hand and spot-checked against the equimolar ternaries, where errors were mostly within about 100-250 K with little mean bias. No search target was asked for; I expect the scale and main trends to be right, with a typical error of roughly 100-200 K on the 4+ element alloys (about 65% confident the RMSE is below 200 K).
