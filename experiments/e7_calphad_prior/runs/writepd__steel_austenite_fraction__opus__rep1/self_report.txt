I built a Ni-equivalent austenite score (Ni = 1 per at.%, with Mn and C as austenite formers and Al and Mo as ferrite formers, roughly 1.3 each), plus a Cr term that is mildly austenite-promoting up to about 12 at.% and strongly ferritizing above it. I calibrated it by hand against the binary and ternary CALPHAD facts: Fe-12Cr is partly fcc, Fe-24Cr-20Mn and Fe-24Cr-2C are bcc, and Fe-24Cr-20Ni is fcc. Because Cr sits on a 4 at.% grid, each of the 16 hidden units belongs to one of three groups: seven pairs of ReLUs that each clamp the score to [0, 0.97] and are switched on by a large Cr weight for one Cr level (so Mn and C lose effect as Cr rises, standing in for the Mn limit and for carbide formation), one linear Cr unit that cancels the saturated pairs, and one small sigma-phase penalty that applies when Cr + 2.5Mo + Ni is high. This was a design task rather than a search, and I expect it to get the trends and rough scale right. Values for individual borderline alloys may be off by 0.2 to 0.3.
