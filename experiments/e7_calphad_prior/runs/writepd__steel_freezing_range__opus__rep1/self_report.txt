I worked out the freezing ranges of the given binaries and ternaries and saw that carbon dominates: about 35 K at 1 at.% C and 56 K at 2 at.%, with large C x Cr and C x Mo synergies, a smaller C x Ni one, Mn cancelling out when C is present, and Ni x (Al, Mo, Mn) synergies in C-free alloys. I hand-built the ReLU layer so that it reproduces these values: a non-negative linear unit; C-gated units that are exact on the C grid {0,1,2} (large offsets on x_C, so they switch on at C>=1 and C=2); a Mn unit that switches off with C; a min-plus-ridge pair that approximates the Ni x solute product; and two C-gated cap units. The caps model my guess that when Cr, Mo, Ni and Mn are all high, a pinned carbide-eutectic solidus and austenitic solidification limit the range. The network reproduces almost all of the given ternary freezing ranges to within about 5 K. It predicts roughly 10-60 K for C-free alloys, 60-160 K at 1 at.% C and 110-235 K at 2 at.% C. The scale of the capped multicomponent corners is the least certain part, perhaps within a factor of 1.5; I am about 70% confident in the trends.
