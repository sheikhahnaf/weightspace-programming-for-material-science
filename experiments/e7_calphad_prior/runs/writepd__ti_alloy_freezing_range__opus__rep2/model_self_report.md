# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I fitted a dilute-solution partition-coefficient model to the binary and ternary facts: freezing range = 2212 K x sum_i c_i (ln k_i)^2, where ln k_i = l0_i + sum_j eps_ij c_j. The fit gives Al-X and Mo-Nb interactions that reproduce all 15 pair freezing ranges to within about 20 K, and it makes Al's own partitioning cancel between the Mo/Nb group and the Sn/V/Zr group. I then encoded each solute's term c_i * phi(|L_i|) as gated product approximations (a min-type unit plus one or two AND-type hinges that switch off when the solute is absent), plus two one-sided Al hinges and one shared linear unit. Beyond the range covered by the pair data, I made the quadratic growth linear to temper the extrapolation. By hand, the network matches the pair facts mostly to within 10-20 K, overestimating weak dilute combinations by up to about 19 K, and it tracks the tempered model to within about 25% on test alloys of four to six components (roughly 20-400 K). I am about 60% confident the trends are right, and less sure of the absolute scale for the most heavily alloyed compositions.
