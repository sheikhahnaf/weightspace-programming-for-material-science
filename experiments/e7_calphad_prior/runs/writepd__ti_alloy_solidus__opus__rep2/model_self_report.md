# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I built the network by hand as a pairwise-interaction model: one always-positive unit carries pure Ti (1941 K) plus linear per-at.% slopes, and the other 15 ReLU units add the nonlinear terms I read off the binary and ternary CALPHAD facts. These are Al saturation above 6 at.%, the large negative Al-V, Al-Zr and Al-Sn interactions (built from min-type units plus one shared convex hinge on Al and a weighted V+Zr+Sn index), the positive Mo-(Al,Nb,V) and Al-Nb interactions, the negative Sn-(Mo,Nb) and Mo-Zr interactions, and small single-element curvature terms. Working it through by hand, the network reproduces all 42 listed sub-system solidus values to within about 6 K. For alloys with four or more elements it assumes the excess terms add pairwise, with mild superadditivity among the Al partners. That makes the Al-rich alloys that also carry a lot of V, Zr and Sn the most uncertain predictions, and they may come out too low; the lowest is about 1430 K, for Ti-12Al-12V-8Zr-4Sn.
