# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I hand-built a hardness model in units of 100 HV. The first layer holds a rule-of-mixtures term with effective per-element hardness values (late 3d metals about 1.2-2, refractories 3.3-5.8, Si/B/C large). It also holds two ReLU hinges for the Al-driven FCC-to-BCC/B2 jump between 10 and 20 at.% Al, the fraction of late 3d metals (L), an intermetallic-former sum (Ti, Nb, Zr, Hf, Ta, Mo, V, W, Si, B, C, Sn) capped at 6L by a ReLU-difference, and an Al-in-refractory term capped at twice the refractory fraction. The second layer adds these into one unit and uses a second unit to cap the Al hinge at 5L, so the large Al jump only applies to 3d-based alloys. I tuned the coefficients by hand against remembered values: CoCrFeNi and Cantor about 145 HV, the AlxCoCrFeNi series from 165 to 520 HV, NbMoTaW about 460 HV, HfNbTaTiZr about 360 HV, and Ti, Nb or Si additions to AlCoCrFeNi about 600-800 HV.
