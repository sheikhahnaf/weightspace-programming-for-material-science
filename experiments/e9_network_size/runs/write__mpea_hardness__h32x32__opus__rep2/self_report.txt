I built the network by hand from known MPEA hardness trends rather than from data. The first layer computes a rule-of-mixtures baseline with per-element effective hardness, in hundreds of HV: soft late 3d metals around 80-190 HV, refractory metals 320-650 HV, and B, C and Si as strong hardeners at 2000-2500. It also computes three nonlinear terms. The first is a min-type "tent" that adds hardness when large early transition metals (Ti, Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta, plus weaker Mo, V, W) mix with a Co/Cr/Cu/Fe/Mn/Ni/Pd matrix, standing in for Laves, sigma and eta precipitation. The second is a ramp of about 280 HV for the FCC-to-BCC transition as x_Al goes from 0.1 to 0.2, switched off in refractory-based alloys. The third is a small Laves term for V/Mo/W mixed with Zr/Hf. The second layer sums these terms and softly caps predictions above 900 HV. I checked the scale by hand against remembered values (CoCrFeMnNi about 139, AlCoCrFeNi about 489, HfNbTaTiZr about 362, NbMoTaW about 507 HV), so I expect the trends and rough scale to be right, with errors of about 100-150 HV on individual alloys.
