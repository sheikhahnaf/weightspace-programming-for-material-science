I built the network by hand from ten active hidden units and left the other 54 at zero. Unit 0 is an always-on rule of mixtures with per-element contributions in HV. All its weights were shifted positive and the output bias of -200 removes the shift; Ti, Nb, Zr and Hf are soft, Mo, W and V hard, and B, C and Si very hard. Two ramps with plateaus model phase-driven hardening. One is a saturating ramp of +450 HV for early transition metals (Ti, Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta, plus weaker weights for Mo, W, V, Re and Sn) dissolved in a 3d matrix; this is Laves or sigma formation, and it is always saturated in refractory alloys. The other is a +330 HV ramp for Al (and half of Si) between about 10% and 20% Al, the FCC-to-BCC/B2 transition in CoCrFeNi-type alloys, which refractory content switches off. Small hinge units saturate the B, C and Si terms and soften alloys rich in Al or other elements. I checked the design by mental arithmetic against remembered values (CoCrFeNi about 160, AlCoCrFeNi about 500, CoCrFeNiNbx, MoNbTaW about 455, HfNbTaTiZr about 370), so I expect the trends to be right and individual errors of roughly 100 to 150 HV.
