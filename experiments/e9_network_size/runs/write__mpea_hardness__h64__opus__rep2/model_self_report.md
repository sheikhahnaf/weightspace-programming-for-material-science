# What the model said it did

Written by the model after its reply; not verified beyond the scored reply itself.

> I built the network by hand from known MPEA hardness trends, using 9 active hidden units with pre-activations in units of 100 HV and output weights of +/-100. Unit 0 is a rule of mixtures with per-element effective hardness values: soft Co/Ni/Cu/Fe/Mn around 100-160 HV, Cr 220, refractory elements 300-650, and Si/B/C as strong hardeners. Units 1-4 add an intermetallic-precipitation bonus for Ti/Nb/Zr/Hf/Ta/Mo/W/V added to a Co/Fe/Ni/Mn/Cu matrix, approximately min(weighted additions, 20*matrix fraction, 6) so that it vanishes in fully refractory alloys and is capped at 600 HV. The other units are a ramp from the FCC-to-BCC/B2 transition at x_Al of 0.10-0.185 (about +300 HV), a softening above x_Al = 0.4, and a Laves bonus for Cr in refractory alloys that have no late transition metals. Hand checks give about 155 HV for CoCrFeNi and CoCrFeMnNi, 500 for AlCoCrFeNi, 480 for NbMoTaWV, 330 for HfNbTaTiZr and 520 for CrNbTiVZr. I am fairly confident in the trends and the rough scale (about 60% that the prior is clearly better than a constant mean), but less so in the exact sizes of the B/C/Si and intermetallic terms.
