I started from the melting point of pure Ni (1728 K) and gave each solute a linear solidus slope taken from the Ni-X binary phase diagrams and my memory of how superalloy solidus changes per at.%. The slopes are: Ti -11, Mo -5.5, Al -5, Cr -2.6, Co -0.2 and W +2 K/at.%. Hidden unit 0 carries this always-positive linear term, scaled by 1000 in the output layer. Unit 1 adds a concave correction: a further 25% drop once the linear drop passes 100 K. Unit 2 adds -10 K per at.% of Al+Ti above 14 at.%, to stand for gamma-prime appearing at the solidus. All other units are zero. Nothing had to be found in this task. I expect the trends to be right, but the absolute scale could be off by roughly 30 to 60 K for heavily alloyed compositions.
