I built a Schaeffler-style austenite stability score S = 1.1 + 19 x_Ni + 9 x_Mn + 60 x_C - 8.5 x_Cr - 20 x_Mo - 30 x_Al. I set its coefficients by thinking through anchor alloys at 900 C: 304/309-type, duplex, 410/420/430-type, medium-Mn Al steels, TWIP Fe-Mn-Al and Fe-Ni-Al. Two ReLUs clip S to [0, 1-P], where P is a small linear cap reduction for carbides (C), sigma/Laves (Mo, Cr) and B2 (Al). A third ReLU subtracts up to about 0.04 for B2 NiAl when both Al and Ni are high. The other 61 hidden units are zero. I expect the trends to be right (Ni, Mn, C raise austenite; Cr, Mo, Al lower it, with most alloys near 0.9-1). The duplex boundary and the secondary-phase fractions are rough, especially for low-Cr Al/Mo alloys and high-Cr/Mo sigma-prone ones.
