I ranked the w0 weights by how much bulk modulus they remove per unit of crystal-to-crystal energy spread (|dK mean| / dE sd), took the 14 best, and solved by hand a small constrained least-squares problem: minimise the summed energy variance (treating the weights as independent) so that the predicted mean bulk-modulus change is -1.0 GPa and the mean energy changes cancel exactly within the edit. That way no shift or E0 change is needed, and any common factor in the energy changes also cancels. The estimated energy spread is about 3 meV per atom (mean absolute change about 2.5 meV). So the correction is deliberately small: it removes about 1 GPa of the +4.7 GPa bias, not all of it, because a larger edit would break the 5 meV budget. I am about 80% confident that the energy constraint holds and about 55% confident that the bulk-modulus MAE on new crystals actually drops, because correlations between features and the shift in equilibrium volume, which the table does not report, could cancel or reverse an effect this small.
