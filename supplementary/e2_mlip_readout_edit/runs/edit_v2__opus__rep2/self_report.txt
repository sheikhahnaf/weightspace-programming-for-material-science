I ranked the w0 weights by how much they move the bulk modulus per unit of crystal-to-crystal energy spread (|dK mean| / dE sd), picked 13 high-ratio, "off-trend" weights (including a few whose K-lowering direction also lowers energy, so the mean energy shifts partly cancel), and sized each change in proportion to dK/sd^2, which is the least-variance allocation if the weights' effects are independent. The combined edit should lower the bulk modulus by about 1.6 GPa on average (roughly a third of the +4.7 GPa bias, larger in absolute terms for stiff crystals if the effect scales with K). It raises the mean energy by about 6.8 meV/atom, which I cancel exactly with shift = -0.0068; this leaves a per-crystal spread of about 3.6 meV (mean absolute about 3 meV) if the effects are independent. This was not a find-something task. I am about 70% confident that the edit both passes the 5 meV energy limit and lowers the bulk-modulus error on new crystals; the main risk is unknown positive correlation between the features' energy effects.
