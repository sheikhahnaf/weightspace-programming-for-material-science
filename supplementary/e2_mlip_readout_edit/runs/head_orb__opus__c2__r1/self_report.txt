I treated the roughly 44 large weights as the only live features (the tiny ones look dead, and their dK values look like fitting noise). I moved each live weight in the direction that stiffens, by an amount proportional to dK_j / sd(dE_j)^2. That ratio is optimal if the energy residuals are independent. I then shifted b by +0.00354 to cancel the mean energy change, about -5.3 meV/atom, so that only the crystal-to-crystal spread remains. This first attempt is deliberately small: it should raise K by about 0.6 GPa on average, its triangle-inequality bound on the energy MAE is about 8 meV, and the energy MAE I expect is 2 to 5 meV. The measured energy MAE will show how correlated the features are, and the later attempts will be rescaled to the 5 meV limit or switched to contrast directions. I am about 75% confident this stays within the energy limit and lowers the bulk-modulus MAE slightly.
