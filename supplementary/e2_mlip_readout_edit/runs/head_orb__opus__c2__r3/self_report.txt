Attempt 2's energy cost (4.42 meV for only +0.5 GPa) matched what you would expect if the 20 large negative-weight features were almost perfectly correlated across crystals. I concluded that moving the live features together cannot do much better. So I switched to the 212 near-zero ("dead") weights, kept those with |dK/dE_sd| >= 40 (98 edited), and set each change proportional to dK_j/sd_j^2. This spreads the energy change over many weakly correlated features. Before that, I took out 90% of the component along each feature's mean-energy/sd. That component lines up strongly with dK and probably carries a coherent factor shared with the large features. I sized the edit so the energy change has a predicted sd of about 4.8 meV (MAE about 3.9 meV if the remaining parts are independent), and set b to cancel the mean energy shift of -1.03 meV. Predicted in-sample bulk-modulus shift: about +2.7 GPa. I am about 50% confident the energy MAE stays under 5 meV, because hidden correlations among the dead features may inflate it. Energy is exactly linear in w, so a later attempt can rescale this direction to the limit.
